**Notation** (as in the other maps). `E.<year>.<name>` = event; `O.<name>` = outcome. `~` = circa, `<` = before, `>` = after (as the 5000 map writes undated rows). `R` = replaceable; `C` = crucial; `NPC (hero)` = a canon hero, always an NPC. All years in this map are ABY, in the ids too (as in the 4–44 ABY map).

## How to read it

**IDs.** Events are `E.<year>.<name>`; outcomes are `O.<name>`. An event *consumes* outcomes (its inputs) and *produces* outcomes that later events consume. That is the network the ripple walks. Year numbers in ids are ABY. Every full id here is checked by script against every other map (docs-v2/spec and outputs/tagmaps): none repeats. Whether to mark ABY ids is still open from the 4–44 ABY map (Part 3, §7).

An outcome is produced at the event's end unless the row says "(produced at its start)" (world-events.md §1). "(optional)" after a consumed outcome means the event goes ahead adjusted if it is broken. "(only while … holds)" marks an outcome produced by its own rule: it comes about only if the named condition is intact (world-events.md §1, `when`).

**Roles.** `R` = replaceable: if the person is gone, someone of similar standing takes over and nothing downstream changes. `C` = crucial: if they are gone, the event diverges.

**Conditions.** Each has a crucial flag and a guard (world-events.md §2):
- **low:** one scene or a single good roll can break it;
- **medium:** a deliberate effort (a mission, an investigation, a won fight);
- **high:** foreknowledge or extraordinary action; needs **one prerequisite** first, named in the condition (a lead recorded in play);
- **very high:** several steps that must each succeed, or a much harder feat; needs **two distinct prerequisites**, (a) and (b), both named.
A prerequisite is always a lead found in play (a figure met, a site found, a fact learned in a scene). Knowledge of the novels, comics or games never counts (D-468).

Every condition says **who** can reach it (Jared's three tests, below). A condition no player could reach is written **Unbreakable**, with the reason, and the earliest tag that can change it instead.

**Undated rows.** The long gap (era 2) and the seven years of Sith rule (era 5) hold rows the lore dates only as "after 45", "before 127" or "between 45 and 130 ABY". They are written `E.>45.…` or `E.<130.…` and their date column says so. The engine settles an event on a date (world-events.md §1), so these rows cannot settle until Jared rules how to place them (Part 3, §7, item 2). Until then they stand for any campaign that starts after their latest bound, and they carry no conditions: no step can be scored against an event with no date or place.

**Standing state.** Whatever happened before the campaign's start date is `standing`. A campaign that starts inside this map treats every earlier row as standing.

**Plot points.** Events marked **[PP-n]** feed a plot point tracker. Each plot point has a hinge, player-reachable steps scored against the three tests, its closed moments, and 2–4 authored alternatives chosen from game state. Everything else diverges under the narrator's direction from the broken tags.

**Where this map joins the others.** It starts where the 4–44 ABY map ends (E.44.liberation, E.44.dorvan, E.44.imperial_election, E.44.jaina_jag) and consumes its outcomes O.one_sith_hidden, O.abeloth_destroyed, O.reige_head_of_state, O.dorvan_chief, O.fel_head_of_state, O.fel_solo_marriage and O.luke_grand_master, and from its earlier eras O.galactic_alliance, O.vong_war_ended, O.ossus_academy, O.new_jedi_temple and O.coruscant_capital (Part 3, §1, link 1). If that map's plot points diverged (no Krayt in hiding, no Fel at the Remnant's head, the Vong not in exile), every event here that consumes the broken outcome waits, is cancelled or is altered by the usual rules. It ends with the Galactic Federation Triumvirate and the start of Darth Wredd's insurgency in 138 ABY; 139 ABY is outside its scope.

## Design rules (Jared, D-489 and the review notes of 24 Nov 2026; apply to every step)

**The three tests.** Every step a player can take is scored against:
1. **There:** is it realistic for a player character to be there?
2. **Standing and power:** what rank, trust and Force or combat power does the player need?
3. **Kind:** what kind of character (Jedi in hiding, Imperial Knight, Fel-loyal or Krayt-loyal Imperial officer, Imperial Intelligence agent, Galactic Alliance Remnant officer, Mon Calamari Ranger, bounty hunter or pirate, Mandalorian, Yuuzhan Vong Shaper, Sith of the One Sith)?
A step that fails test 1 is not a step: the moment is a closed system. Every condition names **who** can reach it.

**Top duelists.** Any fight with a top duelist needs at least a Master. A regular Master only holds one off briefly (Kit Fisto against Sidious): enough to buy a moment, never to win. Beating one, or holding one off for long, needs a Yoda- or Mace-level character: **top tier**. Some beings are **beyond any duelist**: no fight with them can be won as a step, whatever the player is. Proposed for this era (each with its reason; for Jared, Part 3, §7, item 1):
- **Beyond any duelist:**
  - **Darth Krayt** (130–138 ABY). Ch18 puts him at the apex of his age (Tier A, best swordsman alive, near-equal of Luke at his height), and no Yoda- or Mace-level character lives on the other side in his time, so the top tier is out of every player's reach. His two falls come from an ambush by several (Azlyn Rae's stab and Karness Muur's lightning, E.137.had_abbadon) and from Cade, both NPC acts in closed moments.
  - **Celeste Morne under Karness Muur** (127–137 ABY): a Jedi Master possessed by a Sith spirit, with the Muur Talisman and a rakghoul horde; she electrocuted Krayt.
  - **Spirits and entities:** Darth Andeddu's spirit (destroyed 137 ABY), the shadow beings in the Chiloon Rift monolith (45 ABY), and Abeloth if she returns (the Jedi Council expects it; O.abeloth_destroyed).
- **Apex tier (Jared, 25 Nov 2026, D-494, D-495):** above the top duelists: combat 98, Force Control 110, Willpower 105. Yoda and Sidious; the Dark Times Vader, Mace and the freed Revan are just under it. Only another apex beats an apex duelist; a top-tier fighter only buys time; a Master does not even hold one off. On this map: none listed; Krayt is beyond any duelist, and whether Luke (45 ABY) is apex is not ruled yet.
- **Top duelists:**
  - **Luke Skywalker** (45 ABY, until he retires): at the mortal apex (Ch17, Ch18).
  - **Jaina Solo Fel** (45 ABY): she killed Darth Caedus in 41 ABY.
  - **Kol Skywalker** (to 130 ABY): head of the Jedi High Council and the strongest Jedi of his day (Ch18 Master+, the highest Jedi ratings of the era); he held the Sith at Ossus long enough for the shuttles.
  - **Darth Nihl** (130–138 ABY): killed Kol Skywalker and cut down Wolf Sazen at Ossus.
  - **Darth Wyyrlok (III)** (to 138 ABY): a sorcerer more than a blade (Ch18 High Master/legendary in sorcery), but he struck down Krayt (wounded) and his lightning and alchemy put any fight with him in the top bracket.
  - **Cade Skywalker at his height** (from his escape from the Temple of the Sith, 137 ABY): beat Talon and took Nihl's arm in the same fight, fought Krayt and killed him. Before 137 ABY (a pirate on death sticks) he is not a top duelist.
- **Master-level, not top** (a Master can beat them; a Knight only buys time): **Darth Talon** (Ch18 "Elite"; Cade fought her more than once and lived, though she killed the Imperial Knight Elke Vetter); **Roan Fel** (Ch18 "High"; he killed Darth Kruhl alone, but is an Imperial Knight duelist, not a top one); **Antares Draco** and the Imperial Knights' best (Ganner Krieg, Treis Sinde, Azlyn Rae: Ch18 "High"; Draco was taken by Havok); **Darth Stryfe** (he and Wolf Sazen, a Master, killed each other); **Darth Havok** (Eshkar Niin, a former Imperial Knight Master); **Darth Wredd** (Ch18 about the same as Stryfe; he killed his own master by surprise). **Masters, not top:** Wolf Sazen, K'Kruhk, T'ra Saa, Tili Qua, Shado Vao (Knight to Master).

**Non-Force fighters.** In an outright fight a non-Force fighter never reaches Master against a top duelist. Only the most skilled bounty hunters and Mandalorians reach the Master level, and only with planning recorded in play (an ambush, a weapon chosen for the target); they buy time, never win. In this era: the Mandalorians of the Mand'alor Chernan Ordo and Yaga Auchs, Mirta Gev's company (45 ABY), Jariah Syn (an anti-Force weapons specialist), Black Sun's hunters. Morrigan Corde's shot into Krayt's back (E.137.cade_escapes) is the rule at work: a non-Force act from surprise that buys Cade his escape and wins nothing.

**Closed systems.** Rooms and moments no player character would realistically be in, or a character's own choice made alone, are unbreakable and can only be changed earlier. In this map: inside the Chiloon Rift monolith (45); the Moffs' decision to invoke the Treaty of Anaxes as Roan Fel's own reluctant consent (127); Calixte's choice to warn Fel (130); the Moff Council chamber at the Attack on Emperor Fel (130); Cade's dark transfer on Wolf Sazen (130); Krayt's inner chambers while he tempts Cade, and the fight in which Corde shoots him (137); Krayt's orders to execute the Mon Calamari and Wyyrlok's Final Protocol (137); Wyyrlok's murder of Krayt at the foot of the cliff on Had Abbadon (137); Krayt's resurrection and the Assault on the Temple of the Sith (138); Krayt's last duel with Cade, Roan Fel's fall, Antares Draco's oath-kill and Calixte's shot at Veed (138).

**No premonitions.** A step is never justified by a vision, a dream or "a feeling" the narrator gives the player. Canon visions (Kol's guiding vision of the Ossus Project, Cade's vision of his father in the Temple of the Sith, Luke's ghost) are part of the record and are NPCs' motives, never a player's lead. Krayt sensing Cade's dark transfer at Ossus is lore, not a lead a player can have.

**Only the player breaks tags.** History otherwise plays out as recorded. A broken event is never re-staged. An investigation counts only from a lead recorded in play. Canon heroes are NPCs and the player never takes their role: Luke, Leia, Han, Ben Skywalker, Jaina Solo Fel, Jagged Fel, Kol Skywalker, Cade Skywalker, Nat Skywalker (Bantha Rawk), Wolf Sazen, Shado Vao, Jariah Syn, Deliah Blue, Morrigan Corde, Roan and Marasiah Fel, Antares Draco, Azlyn Rae, Gar Stazi, K'Kruhk, Nei Rin, Ania Solo and the rest. A player can stand beside them, be trusted by them, warn them or fight them, never be them. The *Mynock*'s crew is Cade, Jariah and Deliah; a player can be a hireling, a contact or a rival, not a fourth canon crewman. After a divergence each takes a role like their own.

**Belief.** An exposure breaks a condition only if someone with power to act believes it (as in PP-15 of the 5000 map): the player carries the evidence to the Jedi High Council, the Emperor or his Imperial Knights, the Moff Council, the Alliance Triumvirs or Admiral Stazi, and the check uses the player's standing and the strength of the evidence. Belief needs Knight or officer standing or physical proof. A failed check leaves the fact on record as rumour, which a later player can find as a lead.

**Spirits and entities.** Karness Muur, Darth Andeddu's spirit, the Rift monolith's shadow beings and Abeloth act beyond a player's reach. Steps against them work through the people and places they need (a host, a talisman, a body), never through out-fighting the entity.

## 3. The Ossus Project, 122–126 ABY **[PP-36]**

The Mandalorians withdraw after Botajef. The Alliance is winning until the One Sith join through Calixte (128). Coruscant falls, the Alliance surrenders after Caamas, and two days later a diplomatic meeting on Ossus becomes a massacre; Kol dies and Cade is left behind. At the victory council Krayt kills Fel's double and takes the throne; Fel, warned by Calixte, escapes (130) (T45 P1; R-c 5; W: Ossus Project, Massacre at Ossus, Attack on Emperor Fel).
