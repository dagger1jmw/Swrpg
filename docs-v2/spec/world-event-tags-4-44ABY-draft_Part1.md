## How to read it

**IDs.** Events are `E.<year>.<name>`; outcomes are `O.<name>`. An event *consumes* outcomes (its inputs) and *produces* outcomes that later events consume. That is the network the ripple walks. Year numbers in ids are ABY. Because the BBY maps also write plain years (E.19.order66 is 19 BBY), every id here uses a name no other map uses, so no full id clashes (checked by script against all four earlier maps); whether to mark ABY ids is in "Not ruled yet" (Part 3, §7).

An outcome is produced at the event's end unless the row says "(produced at its start)" (world-events.md §1); spans that later events need while they run are marked so. "(optional)" after a consumed outcome means the event goes ahead adjusted if it is broken.

**Roles.** `R` = replaceable: if the person is gone, someone of similar standing takes over and nothing downstream changes. `C` = crucial: if they are gone, the event diverges.

**Conditions.** Each has a crucial flag and a guard (world-events.md §2):
- **low:** one scene or a single good roll can break it;
- **medium:** a deliberate effort (a mission, an investigation, a won fight);
- **high:** foreknowledge or extraordinary action; needs **one prerequisite** first, named in the condition (a lead recorded in play);
- **very high:** several steps that must each succeed, or a much harder feat; needs **two distinct prerequisites**, both named.
A prerequisite is always a lead found in play (a figure met, a site found, a fact learned in a scene). Knowledge of the films, novels, comics or games never counts (D-468).

Most conditions also say **who** can reach them (Jared's three tests, below). A condition no player could reach is written **Unbreakable**, with the reason, and the earliest tag that can change it instead.

**Standing state.** Whatever happened before the campaign's start date is `standing`. A campaign that starts inside this map treats every earlier row as standing.

**Plot points.** Events marked **[PP-n]** feed a plot point tracker. Each plot point has a hinge, player-reachable steps scored against the three tests, its unbreakable moments, and 2–4 authored alternatives chosen from game state. Everything else diverges under the narrator's direction from the broken tags.

**Where this map joins the others.** It starts where the 1000 BBY – 4 ABY map ends (E.4.endor, E.4.fragment, E.4.new_republic) and consumes its outcomes: O.emperor_dead, O.empire_fragmented, O.new_republic, O.alliance, O.han_free, O.luke_trained, O.twins_hidden. It also reads O.hapes_isolated (3640 map, E.3100.hapes) and O.kun_spirit_bound (5000 map, E.3996.yavin). If PP-8 diverged (the Emperor survives Endor, or no Endor), every event here is narrator-directed until a later ruling: this map assumes the recorded Endor. It ends at the close of 44 ABY; *Crucible* (45 ABY) and the Legacy era belong to the next map, which should consume O.one_sith_hidden, O.reige_head_of_state, O.fel_solo_marriage, O.dorvan_chief and O.abeloth_destroyed (Part 3, §1, link 14).

## Design rules (Jared, 24 Nov 2026; apply to every step)

**The three tests.** Every step a player can take is scored against:
1. **There:** is it realistic for a player character to be there?
2. **Standing and power:** what rank, trust and Force or combat power does the player need?
3. **Kind:** what kind of character (Jedi, pilot, intelligence agent, Imperial officer, smuggler, senator's aide, Yuuzhan Vong-side collaborator, Sith of the Lost Tribe)?
A step that fails test 1 is not a step: the moment is a closed system.

**Top duelists.** Any fight with a top duelist needs at least a Master. A regular Master only holds one off briefly (Kit Fisto against Sidious). Beating one, or holding one off for long, needs a Yoda- or Mace-level character. In this era the top duelists are: **Luke Skywalker at his height** (from his return from Byss in 10 ABY; Ch14–Ch17 put him near-apex from then and at the mortal apex by 43 ABY), **Palpatine reborn**, **Exar Kun's spirit**, **Lumiya**, **Darth Caedus** (and Jacen Solo from 29 ABY, "second only to Luke"), **Lomi Plo**, **Darth Krayt**, and, beyond any duelist, **Abeloth** (no mortal fights her alone; it took Luke and Krayt together). Similar: Joruus C'baoth (raw power, not a duelist, but a Master-level threat), Kyp Durron under Kun, Jerec and Desann (High Master tier). A player cannot "win the fight" against any of these as a step unless they are of that tier themselves.

**Closed systems.** Rooms and moments no player character would realistically be in are unbreakable and can only be changed earlier. In this map: Palpatine's spirit passing to Byss at Endor; the throne room on Byss (10 ABY); the *Chimaera*'s bridge when Rukh strikes (9 ABY); Luke's fight with Kun's spirit around his own body; Jacen and Mara alone on Kavan (40 ABY); Jaina and Caedus aboard the *Anakin Solo* (41 ABY); the realm "beyond shadows" where Luke and Krayt fight Abeloth (44 ABY); and Abeloth's own acts at a distance (the psychosis).

**No premonitions.** A step is never justified by a vision, a dream or "a feeling" the narrator gives the player: that would be the narrator changing the story. Canon visions (Jacen's flow-walking, Luke's foresight, the Mind Walkers' vision) are part of the record and are NPCs' motives, never a player's lead. The Yuuzhan Vong are invisible to the Force (Ch15): no Jedi, player or not, senses the invasion coming or a Vong agent in a room.

**Only the player breaks tags.** History otherwise plays out as recorded. A broken event is never re-staged. An investigation counts only from a lead recorded in play. Canon heroes are NPCs and the player never takes their role: Luke, Leia, Han, Chewbacca, Lando, Mara Jade, Wedge, Corran Horn, Kyle Katarn, Jaden Korr, Talon Karrde, Kyp Durron, Jacen, Jaina and Anakin Solo, Tenel Ka, Ben Skywalker, Vestara Khai, Jagged Fel, Tahiri Veila, Zekk, Raynar Thul and the rest. A player can stand beside them, be trusted by them, warn them or fight them, never be them. After a divergence each takes a role like their own.

**Spirits and entities.** Exar Kun's spirit, Ragnos's spirit and Abeloth act beyond a player's reach. Steps against them work through the people and places they need (a student alone, a body, a prison), never through out-fighting the entity.
