# Lore decisions: 4–25 ABY and 5,000–3,997 BBY (open items)

Recommendations for the items left open in `lore-rulings-2026-11-24b.md` ("Still open"). Researched 24 Nov 2026 from Wookieepedia (Legends) through the API. Each item gives one recommended ruling the developer can accept with a "yes". Rulings already made in `lore-rulings-2026-11-24b.md` are not reopened. Confidence: **Clear** (the sources settle it), **Likely** (best reading, small residual doubt), **Needs Jared** (a story choice).

## 4–25 ABY (`SW_Timeline_4_25ABY`)

### Item 4 — Name of the 4–12 ABY war

**Recommendation:** Call it the **Imperial Civil War** (4–12 ABY), with "Imperial Mutiny", "War of Purification" and "Time of Destruction" as in-universe alternate names. Drop "First Imperial Civil War" as a proper name; the old file's "4–5 ABY" span is replaced by 4 ABY (shortly after Endor) to 12 ABY (Daala's execution of the warlords at Tsoss Beacon).

**Why:** Wookieepedia's Legends article is titled "Imperial Civil War" and lists exactly those alternate names; no page titled "First Imperial Civil War" exists. The article's hatnote describes it only descriptively as "the first Imperial Civil War" to tell it apart from the **Second Imperial Civil War** (130–138 ABY, *Legacy*), whose page notes it was also simply called "the Imperial Civil War". If the game ever reaches the Legacy era, "first" can be used in prose as a description, not as the name.

**Sources:** [Imperial Civil War](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Imperial_Civil_War) (lead and infobox: begin 4 ABY per *The New Essential Chronology*, end 12 ABY per *Darksaber*); [Second Imperial Civil War](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Second_Imperial_Civil_War) (lead, 130–138 ABY).

**Confidence:** Clear

### Item 6 — The Keeramak: 14 ABY or 23 ABY?

**Recommendation:** Keep both as two steps. **14 ABY:** the Keeramak, the mutant Ssi-ruu bearing every caste colour, comes to power on Lwhekk as Grand Shreeftut. **22–23 ABY:** the Imperium's leadership, covertly steered by the Yuuzhan Vong infiltrator E'thinaa, stages a faux P'w'eck slave uprising (the P'w'eck Emancipation Movement under Lwothin) with the Keeramak as its figurehead; the cover story, told to Bakura in 28 ABY, is that this revolt took Lwhekk and toppled the Imperium. Do not use "the Keeramak was installed as Shreeftut only in 23 ABY".

**Why:** The 14 ABY year page cites *Force Heretic II: Refugee* for the Keeramak coming to power, and the Keeramak's own page gives its Grand Shreeftut tenure as 14–28 ABY; its biography has it elected Shreeftut before the Bakura plan. The 23 ABY date on the Ssi-ruuvi invasions page is only for the staged uprising "positing the Keeramak as its leader", which fits a ruler already in place. The one page that makes the 23 ABY revolt the Keeramak's route to power is "Keeramak Revolt", a conjecture-titled article whose key paragraph is uncited and which itself says the revolt was a manipulated sham; Lwothin's "within a year Lwhekk was ours… five years on" (spoken in 28 ABY) is part of that cover story and dates the staged revolt, not the Keeramak's accession.

**Sources:** [14 ABY/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/14_ABY/Legends) (Events, cited to *Force Heretic II: Refugee*); [Keeramak](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Keeramak) (Biography; succession box 14–28 ABY); [Ssi-ruuvi invasions](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Ssi-ruuvi_invasions) (lead); [Keeramak Revolt](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Keeramak_Revolt) (lead, Details); [P'w'eck Emancipation Movement](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/P%27w%27eck_Emancipation_Movement).

**Confidence:** Likely (the novel's exact wording could not be checked; if Jared prefers the single cleaner date, the fallback is "Keeramak rules from 23 ABY", but that rests on an uncited conjecture page).

### Item 9 — Cronal's c. 15 ABY death: keep or drop?

**Recommendation:** Keep it as Legends. Cronal survives his apparent death at Mindor (5 ABY) through the dark side; c. 15 ABY, styling himself "Perek", he sets up the "Empire of Art" on Andooweel and is killed there by Luke Skywalker. Tag the c. 15 ABY date and the Perek = Cronal identification to *The Imperial Warlords: Despoilers of an Empire*, Part 3.

**Why:** The source rules define Legends as what Wookieepedia classifies as Legends, and Wookieepedia uses the article on its Legends pages (Cronal's infobox, Andooweel/Legends, Empire of Art). It is official Lucasfilm material (StarWars.com, by Abel G. Peña and Daniel Wallace) published 7 January 2014, before the April 2014 rebrand, so it is pre-2014 Legends, not a gray-area or Canon source. The death itself is older still: it is the plot of the 2004 comic "Collapsing New Empires" (*Star Wars Tales* 19), where Luke kills Perek on Andooweel; the 2014 article only dated it and revealed Perek was Cronal.

**Sources:** [Cronal](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cronal) (infobox: deaths 5 ABY Mindor and c. 15 ABY Andooweel); [The Imperial Warlords: Despoilers of an Empire](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/The_Imperial_Warlords:_Despoilers_of_an_Empire) (release dates); [Collapsing New Empires](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Collapsing_New_Empires) (lead); [Andooweel/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Andooweel/Legends) (History); [Empire of Art](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Empire_of_Art).

**Confidence:** Clear

### Item 12 — Which body replaced the Provisional Council?

**Recommendation:** The **New Republic Provisional Council** (led by Chief Councilor Mon Mothma) stays the governing council until **11 ABY**; after Coruscant is taken in 6 ABY it simply moves its permanent seat there. In **11 ABY**, after Operation Shadow Hand, it is replaced by the **New Republic Ruling Council** (the Chief of State plus the chairs of the Senate councils), and the Chief Councilor post becomes Chief of State. Rewrite the 6/7 ABY row to "the New Republic takes Coruscant, makes it its capital and the Provisional Council's seat, and formally becomes the galaxy's authority"; drop "replaced the Provisional Council with a Senate, General Ministry and Court of Justice" from that row.

**Why:** The Provisional Council's own page says it stayed mobile until Coruscant fell in 6.5 ABY, after which Coruscant became its permanent seat, and that it was dissolved after Shadow Hand and reorganised in 11 ABY as the Ruling Council (*The New Essential Chronology*; *Shield of Lies*). The Thrawn trilogy (9 ABY) shows the Provisional Council still governing, which the 6 ABY reading cannot fit. The 6 ABY year page's "replacing the Provisional Council" clause sits at the end of a sentence whose citations (*The Krytos Trap*, *Essential Atlas*) support the capture of Coruscant, not the reform.

**Sources:** [New Republic Provisional Council](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/New_Republic_Provisional_Council) (lead, infobox, Appearances); [New Republic Ruling Council](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/New_Republic_Ruling_Council) (infobox: founded 11 ABY); [New Republic/Legends §Reorganization and reform](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/New_Republic/Legends); [6 ABY/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/6_ABY/Legends) (Events); [11 ABY/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/11_ABY/Legends).

**Confidence:** Clear

## 5,000–3,997 BBY (`SW_Timeline_5000_3997BBY`)

### Item 2 — Primus Goluud: solar flares or supernova?

**Recommendation:** Both, in sequence: at the Battle at Primus Goluud (5,000 BBY) Sadow uses the Sith-crystal superweapon aboard the *Corsair* to destabilise the red supergiant; its flares erupt and the star goes supernova, destroying the abandoned meditation sphere with Gav Daragon aboard and wrecking the system. Gav's warning lets the Koros fleet escape first.

**Why:** Both versions cite the same comic (*The Fall of the Sith Empire* 4). The battle page says Sadow "trigger[ed] Primus Goluud's solar flares", but links the weapon to "Supernova (Sith magic)" and files the battle under Supernovas; the star's own page says it was "forced into a supernova" by Sadow's superweapon, "which destroyed the system", and files it under Destroyed stars; the war article says Sadow imploded the star and it went supernova. Flares leading into a supernova reconciles all three.

**Sources:** [Battle at Primus Goluud](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_at_Primus_Goluud) (lead, The battle); [Primus Goluud/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Primus_Goluud/Legends); [Great Hyperspace War/Legends §Sadow undone](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Great_Hyperspace_War/Legends); [Supernova (Sith magic)](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Supernova_(Sith_magic)).

**Confidence:** Clear

### Item 4 — Red Sith Purge: 45 or 57?

**Recommendation:** **45** are killed. "The Fifty-seven" is the name of the group: the 57 non-Human Sith who came down from the mountains after the *Omen* crash in 5,000 BBY. Twelve had died of misfortune by 4,985 BBY, and the purge killed the remaining 45 in one night, sparing only the Houk Gloyd.

**Why:** The Red Sith Purge page (from *Lost Tribe of the Sith: Paragon*) gives both figures and explains them: 57 survived the mountains, twelve died by 4,985 BBY, and 45 were purged. Tilden Kaah's line about Seelah ridding the Skyborn of "the Fifty-seven" uses the group's name, which is why other pages carry "Fifty-seven". Small wording fix: the 45 are "the non-Human Sith (mostly purebloods)", since the cyborg Hestus was among them.

**Sources:** [Red Sith Purge](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Sith_Purge) (infobox, lead, Prelude, The purge).

**Confidence:** Clear

### Item 5 — Nida Korsin "heirless" despite her son Donellan

**Recommendation:** No conflict. Donellan (born 4,960 BBY) was her heir but died of old age before her; Nida outlived him and then replaced dynastic succession with succession by merit. She died heirless in 4,896 BBY after a 79-year reign. Rewrite the 4,896 BBY row to say so and drop its [unclear] tag. Optional: the Lost Tribe's "Donellan's Day" marks his birth as a warning against waiting too long to make a move.

**Why:** Nida Korsin's Grand Lord section (citing *Lost Tribe of the Sith: Purgatory*) says she outlived her son, "who had waited too long to make his move", and, heirless, abolished the dynastic system. Donellan's page agrees that he died an old man before he could lead the tribe.

**Sources:** [Nida Korsin](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nida_Korsin) (lead, §Grand Lord); [Donellan](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Donellan); [4960 BBY](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/4960_BBY).

**Confidence:** Clear

### Item 6 — Barabel War: averted dispute or millennia-long conflict?

**Recommendation:** Both describe one thing: a hunting-ground dispute among the Barabel tribes of Barab I that begins c. 4,500 BBY and simmers for millennia without becoming open war, until the Ithorian Jedi Knight Noga-ta settles it c. 902 BBY with a treaty (each tribe gives its night's largest kill to a neighbour). Later Barabels call it "the Barabel War" although no battle was fought, and they revere Jedi because of it. Reword the c. 4,500 BBY rows to "a long dispute begins", and add the c. 902 BBY settlement to the later timeline set.

**Why:** The Barabel War page's own infobox gives a begin date of c. 4,500 BBY (*The New Essential Guide to Alien Species*) and an end of c. 902 BBY (*The Complete Star Wars Encyclopedia*, Noga-ta entry), so the "millennia-long" span is real; its text says open combat never began. Noga-ta's page agrees that he ended the conflict "before it began". The only mismatch is the word "conflict" on the timeline, which the dates show means a long dispute.

**Sources:** [Barabel War](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Barabel_War) (infobox, History); [Noga-ta](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Noga-ta) (Biography).

**Confidence:** Clear

### Item 7 — What Nadd met on Yavin 4 in 4,400 BBY

**Recommendation:** Nadd finds **Sadow himself, alive in a suspended-animation chamber** where he had placed himself to wait for an apprentice. Nadd wakes him, learns his Sith secrets, then kills him, and with him destroys Sadow's spirit. Drop the timeline's "met Sadow's spirit" wording. Note for later sets: SWTOR material (*Shadow of Revan*) still shows Sadow's spirit; treat that as a separate conflict when the SWTOR era is filed.

**Why:** Sadow's Legends page (*The New Essential Chronology*; *New Essential Guide to Characters*; SWTOR codex) has him enter suspended animation that kept his body alive for centuries, be woken by Nadd c. 4,400 BBY, train him and be killed by him; Nadd's page and the 4,400 BBY year page agree. The page's Inconsistencies section explains the "spirit" version: *The Complete Star Wars Encyclopedia* and most later sources say Nadd destroyed Sadow's spirit when he killed him. Body awakened, then body and spirit destroyed, fits both.

**Sources:** [Naga Sadow/Legends §Flight, exile and death; §Inconsistencies](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Naga_Sadow/Legends); [Freedon Nadd](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Freedon_Nadd); [4400 BBY](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/4400_BBY).

**Confidence:** Clear (for 4,400 BBY; the SWTOR ghost is flagged for later)

### Item 13 — The timeline's unreferenced items

**Recommendation:** Treat them one by one, using each subject's own page:
- **Star Cabal:** keep at c. 5,000 BBY, founded after the Great Hyperspace War with the Koros Covenant to rid the galaxy of Jedi and Sith; dissolved 3,640 BBY. Tag [unclear] (the founding line in its infobox carries no footnote).
- **Exchange:** keep as "active from about 4,000 BBY" (the crime syndicate of KOTOR, KOTOR II and SWTOR), not "founded" in that year. Tag [unclear].
- **Muzara:** keep: Humans from Brentaal colonise it in 4,000 BBY under the Brentaal League (*Tales of the Jedi Companion*).
- **Omwati colony on Coruscant:** drop the c. 4,000 BBY date. The Omwati page says only that, at some point in the remote past, Republic ships took Omwati from Omwat to a ghetto on Coruscant. Keep it as undated background. An Omwati Jedi, Tannis, is attested c. 4,000 BBY, so Omwati were already off-world by then.
- **Ession:** drop. Its page says it had been settled since before 6,000 BBY (*Essential Atlas*), so the c. 4,000 BBY colonisation contradicts the stronger source.
- **Flashpoint Stellar Research Station:** drop the c. 4,000 BBY founding. Its page gives no founding date, only its capture in 3,964 BBY and destruction in 3,963 BBY.
- **Slivilith:** drop the 3,997 BBY date. Keep "Yuuzhan Vong slivilith scouts begin to appear in the galaxy during the Old Sith Wars era (from 4,000 BBY); only four sightings are confirmed in 4,000 years" (*Alien Anthology*).

**Why:** Each subject has a Legends page that either supports the timeline item (Star Cabal, Exchange, Muzara), gives no date (Omwati, Flashpoint, slivilith), or contradicts it (Ession). The source rules say uncited facts don't go in. Where a subject's own page confirms the date, it is no longer unreferenced; where it doesn't, the date goes.

**Sources:** [Star Cabal](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Star_Cabal); [Exchange](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Exchange); [Muzara](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Muzara); [Omwati/Legends §History, §Omwati in the galaxy](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Omwati/Legends); [Ession](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Ession); [Flashpoint Stellar Research Station](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Flashpoint_Stellar_Research_Station); [Slivilith §History](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Slivilith); [Timeline of galactic history/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Timeline_of_galactic_history/Legends).

**Confidence:** Likely (each line is Clear on its own page; Star Cabal and Exchange start dates rest on uncited infobox or lead wording)

### Item 14 — Splitting the old "Great Sith War 3,997–3,996 BBY" block

**Recommendation:** Yes, split it. File the 3,997 BBY rows (Krath coup on Empress Teta, Koros Major, Deneba, Ulic's fall, Kun's rise, the Brotherhood of the Sith) under **"Krath Holy Crusade (3,997–3,996 BBY)"**, and start **"Great Sith War (3,996 BBY)"** with the 3,996 BBY rows, noting that the Krath Holy Crusade runs on alongside it. Both sit under the Old Sith Wars era, which ruling 6 already starts in 4,000 BBY. (Part 2 of the new set already does this, so this ruling only confirms it and retires the old block.)

**Why:** *The New Essential Chronology* dates the Krath Holy Crusade 3,997–3,996 BBY and the Great Sith War to 3,996 BBY only; the Great Sith War infobox lists the Krath Holy Crusade as its predecessor and as concurrent (*Essential Atlas*), and its lead calls the Great Sith War "the first conflict of the Old Sith Wars". A block called "Great Sith War 3,997–3,996" puts a year under the war that no source gives it.

**Sources:** [Krath Holy Crusade](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Krath_Holy_Crusade) (infobox, lead); [Great Sith War/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Great_Sith_War/Legends) (infobox, lead); [Old Sith Wars](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Sith_Wars).

**Confidence:** Clear
