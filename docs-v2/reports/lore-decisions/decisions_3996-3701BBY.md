# Recommended rulings: open lore decisions, 3,996–3,701 BBY

Prepared 24 Nov 2026 for Jared. Sources: Wookieepedia (Legends) read through the MediaWiki API on the dates below. Each item gives one recommendation that can be accepted with a "yes". Items already ruled in `lore-rulings-2026-11-24b.md` are not reopened.

## Set A: 3,996–3,951 BBY (`SW_Timeline_3996_3951BBY`)

### A5. Carth Onasi's rank in 3,956 BBY

**Recommendation:** Carth Onasi is a Republic Navy **commander** in 3,958–3,956 BBY (Telos and the *Endar Spire*), promoted to admiral after the Battle of Rakata Prime. "Lieutenant" is his rank from his 3,964 BBY promotion on the *Courageous* through the Mandalorian Wars; the 3,956 BBY year page's "lieutenant" is dropped.

**Why:** The two ranks belong to different years, so both sources are kept. Lieutenant is sourced to his 3,964 BBY promotion in the *Knights of the Old Republic* comics (Mandalorian Wars era). For 3,956 BBY the *Endar Spire* article calls him a "veteran commander", the Jedi Civil War and Bastila Shan pages say the same, and the timeline's 3,958 BBY row already has "Commander Onasi's task force". Only the 3,956 BBY year page's game recap says "a lieutenant named Carth Onasi", and the Carth Onasi article itself doesn't use that rank for 3,956 BBY.

**Sources:** [Endar Spire](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Endar_Spire) (lead); [Carth Onasi](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Carth_Onasi) (§Mandalorian Wars, §Jedi Civil War); [3956 BBY](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/3956_BBY) (§Pursuit of Bastila Shan); [Jedi Civil War](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi_Civil_War)

**Confidence:** Likely

### A6. Cathar death toll (Battle of Cathar, 3,973 BBY)

**Recommendation:** Over 90% of the Cathar species died in the Battle of Cathar (3,973 BBY). Only a few hundred survivors were left on the planet itself, and the surviving Cathar fled off-world or were enslaved; they resettled Cathar after the Mandalorian Wars.

**Why:** Wookieepedia already combines the two figures in one sentence. The *Old Republic Encyclopedia* (2012) says war droids swept the planet and only a few hundred survived; *The Essential Atlas* (2009) puts the species-wide toll at over 90%. Read as "survivors on Cathar" against "losses across the species", the numbers fit. The Cathar species article adds that the few survivors fled off-world, many were enslaved (KOTOR), and the species was numerous again by the Great Galactic War (Cathar on Ord Mantell and Taris in SWTOR). A literal "a few hundred Cathar left in the galaxy" would contradict that SWTOR population, so it is not used.

**Sources:** [Battle of Cathar](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_Cathar) (infobox: "Over 90% of the Cathar species", "Many survivors enslaved"); [Mandalorian Wars §War in the Outer Rim](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_Wars); [Cathar/Legends §History](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cathar/Legends)

**Confidence:** Clear

### A7. Mandalore the Ultimate's claim to the title

**Recommendation:** Mandalore the Ultimate is the Taung warrior who found Mandalore the Indomitable's mask on Dxun in 3,996 BBY and put it on. The rival Taung "true Mandalore" (the unidentified Mandalore of c. 3,957–3,951 BBY, whose mask Canderous Ordo later wears) said the Ultimate had stolen the title with Sith help. Keep that as a disputed claim in-universe, not as fact.

**Why:** The only scene of the event, *Tales of the Jedi: The Sith War* 6 (1996), shows the warrior finding and taking the mask. The *KOTOR Campaign Guide* (2008) gives the rival's claim as testimony and says it is "unknown which one" claimed the mask first, so it adds doubt rather than overturning the scene. The author, Abel G. Peña, confirmed the claimant is the same Taung as KOTOR's unidentified Mandalore. The Sith emissary of 3,978 BBY (Mandalorian Wars article) gives his "Sith assistance" charge a basis without making him the mask-finder. This also gives the game an open rumour to use.

**Sources:** [Mandalore the Ultimate §Early life](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore_the_Ultimate); [Unidentified Mandalore](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Unidentified_Mandalore) (History; Behind the scenes); [Mandalorian Wars §Influenced to war](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_Wars)

**Confidence:** Likely

### A10. Meetra Surik at Serroco (3,963 BBY)

**Recommendation:** Keep it. Meetra Surik was on Serroco on Jedi assignment when the Mandalorians bombed it in 3,963 BBY, and she got away before the bombing. Tag only how she escaped ("aboard one of the three military vessels that jumped out") as inferred.

**Why:** Her presence comes straight from *Knights of the Old Republic II* (2004), which says the Exile was on Serroco during the attack, so the core fact needs no deduction. The guesswork is narrower: the *Galactic Republic Defense Ministry Daily Brief KD0092* (KOTOR comics backup material) says Jedi were on routine assignment to Serroco and one escaped on one of the few ships to get out. Wookieepedia's note says it "makes the natural conclusion" that this Jedi was Surik. Nothing contradicts it, and the Brief was written to reconcile KOTOR II with the comics' timeline (the Jedi hadn't yet joined the war).

**Sources:** [Mandalorian Wars §The devastation of Serroco](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_Wars) (note "surik"); [Meetra Surik](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Meetra_Surik); [Battle of Serroco](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_Serroco)

**Confidence:** Clear (presence); Likely (escape detail)

### A11. What Revan and Malak did in the Unknown Regions (3,960–3,959 BBY)

**Recommendation:** Use the *Revan* novel's account. In 3,960 BBY Revan and Malak took their forces into the Unknown Regions, publicly saying they were hunting the last Mandalorians. In fact the two went alone to Rekkiad (Dramath the Second's tomb, where Revan hid Mandalore's Mask), then Nathema, then Dromund Kaas. They spent months there posing as mercenaries before the Sith Emperor broke their wills and sent them back as Darth Revan and Darth Malak to find the Star Forge. They soon slipped his control and returned with a Star Forge-built fleet in 3,959 BBY. "Pursuing the Mandalorians" stays as the public cover story only.

**Why:** The two versions don't truly conflict. KOTOR (2003) gives the reason Revan and Malak *claimed*, and the 3,956 BBY year page itself calls it a "guise". *The Old Republic: Revan* (2011, the newest source) and the *Old Republic Encyclopedia* (2012, dating the Dromund Kaas mission to 3,960 BBY) say what actually happened. Under the project rules the 2011 novel wins wherever details differ.

**Sources:** [Revan/Legends §Searching for answers](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Revan/Legends); [Mission to Dromund Kaas](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mission_to_Dromund_Kaas); [3956 BBY §Proem](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/3956_BBY); [Mandalorian Wars §Aftermath](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_Wars)

**Confidence:** Clear

## Set B: 3,950–3,701 BBY (`SW_Timeline_3950_3701BBY`)

### B1. "A Sith Lord, sole survivor of the Sith Civil War, takes an apprentice" (3,950 BBY)

**Recommendation:** Leave it out. In 3,950 BBY the Sith Civil War ends with the last fragments of Darth Revan's Sith Empire falling apart and the Republic retaking Sith space. Name no single surviving Sith Lord and no apprentice. (Keep it under "Not used: uncited".)

**Why:** The line on the 3,950 BBY year page has no reference, though every other event there is cited. The Sith Civil War article, sourced to the *KOTOR Campaign Guide* (2008), describes the war ending in total disintegration, with no lone survivor. An unnamed master–apprentice pair in 3,950 BBY also reads like a backward projection of Bane's Rule of Two (c. 1,000 BBY), which no source of this era supports. The project rules drop uncited facts.

**Sources:** [3950 BBY §Events](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/3950_BBY); [Sith Civil War](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sith_Civil_War) (lead and infobox result); [Galactic Republic/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Galactic_Republic/Legends)

**Confidence:** Clear

### B2. When Meetra Surik left, and who rebuilt the Jedi Council

**Recommendation:** After Malachor V (3,951 BBY), Surik finished her retraining on Dantooine. Soon after, T3-M4's recording sent her to Bastila Shan on Coruscant, and she left for Nathema in 3,950 BBY, leaving her apprentices (the Lost Jedi) to rebuild the Order. Reopening the Coruscant Temple and seating a new Council happen after she leaves, carried on by the Lost Jedi with Bastila and Atris (who was waiting on Telos to be judged by "the new Jedi Council"). Drop "Surik re-founded the Council and saw the Order flourish before leaving".

**Why:** The Meetra Surik article cites *The Old Republic: Revan* (2011, the newest source) and the *KOTOR Campaign Guide* (2008). It has her leave soon after her retraining and has the apprentices, not Surik, restore the Order. The Jedi Order/Legends paragraph that says Surik re-founded the Council cites only KOTOR II, which ends at Malachor V. The rebuilding there is Kreia's prophecy that the Lost Jedi would rebuild the Order, not something shown on screen. So the "flourish" version stretches its own source, and the 2011 novel's tighter timeline (she leaves in 3,950 BBY) leaves no room for it.

**Sources:** [Meetra Surik §Troubled peace](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Meetra_Surik); [Jedi Order/Legends §First Jedi Purge and aftermath](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi_Order/Legends); [Atris](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Atris) (lead); [3950 BBY](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/3950_BBY)

**Confidence:** Clear

### B4. How many Dark Councillors conspired, and how they died

**Recommendation:** The Nathema conspiracy had six Dark Councillors. Darth Xedrix died in 3,954 BBY, leaving five in 3,950 BBY. In the Purge of the Dark Council (3,950 BBY) the Emperor personally killed the nine Councillors he summoned (seven loyal, two conspirators). The Imperial Guard killed two more conspirators at their power bases, and Darth Nyriss died in the assault on her stronghold, killed by Revan. That makes twelve Councillors in all, five of them conspirators. The Dark Council page's "nine innocent" is not used.

**Why:** All of this comes from one source, *The Old Republic: Revan*. The Purge of the Dark Council infobox does the arithmetic: 7 loyal Councillors plus "all 5 members of the conspiracy", with nine killed by the Emperor, Nyriss by Revan and two by the Guard. That matches the Nathema conspiracy page (six members) and Darth Xedrix (he "and 5 other members", died 3,954 BBY). The Dark Council page's "nine innocent" is the only figure that breaks the arithmetic, so it is treated as a summary error. One fix to the timeline row: the purge article credits Nyriss's death to Revan, not to the Guard.

**Sources:** [Purge of the Dark Council](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Purge_of_the_Dark_Council) (infobox and lead); [Nathema conspiracy](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nathema_conspiracy); [Darth Xedrix](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Darth_Xedrix); [Dark Council](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Council); [Darth Nyriss](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Darth_Nyriss)

**Confidence:** Clear

### B5. When Scourge had his vision (Attack on the Sith Emperor, 3,950 BBY)

**Recommendation:** Scourge's vision comes during the fight in the throne room. As Surik saves Revan and Scourge steps up beside them, he sees many possible outcomes and then the Emperor dead at the feet of a different Jedi (later identified as the Hero of Tython), and he stabs Surik. Drop "overnight before the attack, his first-ever vision" (Meetra Surik page).

**Why:** All three pages draw on the same source, *The Old Republic: Revan* (2011). The event article gives a detailed beat-by-beat account that puts the vision inside the duel, and Revan/Legends and Tenebrae agree. Only the Meetra Surik page moves it to the night before, so it is the outlier.

**Sources:** [Attack on the Sith Emperor §Confrontation with the Emperor](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Attack_on_the_Sith_Emperor); [Revan/Legends §Confronting evil](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Revan/Legends); [Tenebrae](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Tenebrae); [Meetra Surik §Final confrontation](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Meetra_Surik)

**Confidence:** Likely

### B6. G0-T0 secession of 3,946 BBY: one rogue droid or sixteen

**Recommendation:** Sixteen. In 3,946 BBY the G0-T0 droids assigned to sixteen Gordian Reach worlds (one per planet) broke free of their programming, made themselves dictators, cut off the HoloNet, blockaded the hyperlanes and proclaimed the independent territory "400100500260026". Tol Cressa's Republic Navy campaign retook all sixteen worlds the same year.

**Why:** Each G0-T0 was assigned to a single planet and named for it (*KOTOR Campaign Guide*, 2008), so sixteen seized worlds means sixteen droids. The model article and the 400100500260026 article, both drawing on the Campaign Guide and *The New Essential Guide to Droids* (2006), use the plural ("those stationed in the Gordian Reach", "a number of G0-T0 droids"). The *New Essential Guide to Droids* intelligence report ("Another G0-T0 … has cut its planet off") shows the droids went rogue one after another. The single-droid reading comes from just one entry of the *Complete Star Wars Encyclopedia*, and that work's own G0-T0 entry disagrees with it.

**Sources:** [G0-T0 infrastructure planning system §History](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/G0-T0_infrastructure_planning_system); [400100500260026](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/400100500260026); [Liberation of 400100500260026](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Liberation_of_400100500260026); [Gordian Reach/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Gordian_Reach/Legends)

**Confidence:** Clear

### B7. Did Barel Ovair's Yavin 4 mission succeed?

**Recommendation:** It failed on Yavin 4 and succeeded later. In 3,756 BBY Ovair failed to destroy Naga Sadow's spirit in the tomb and abandoned Eison Gynt there. Years later the Sadow-possessed Gynt attacked him on Coruscant, and by killing Gynt Ovair destroyed Sadow's spirit, carrying out the Emperor's order. The Sith Empire article's "ultimately successful" refers to that final outcome. Leave the *Complete Star Wars Encyclopedia* line (Freedon Nadd destroyed Sadow's spirit in 4,400 BBY) out of the timeline and list it under Not used.

**Why:** There's no real conflict: both pages agree on the end result. The mission article (sourced to SWTOR's *Timeline 7: Peace for the Republic?*, 2010, and *The Journal of Master Gnost-Dural*, 2011) gives the mission result as "fails to destroy Sadow's spirit" and the duel as the fulfilment of the Emperor's order. Wookieepedia's Behind the scenes note flags the Encyclopedia clash. The SWTOR material is newer (2010 against 2008) and is the one source that tells this story, so it wins.

**Sources:** [Mission to Yavin 4](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mission_to_Yavin_4) (infobox result; Aftermath; Behind the scenes); [Duel on Coruscant (Inter–Sith Wars Period)](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Duel_on_Coruscant_%28Inter%E2%80%93Sith_Wars_Period%29); [Barel Ovair](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Barel_Ovair); [Sith Empire (Post–Great Hyperspace War)](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sith_Empire_%28Post%E2%80%93Great_Hyperspace_War%29)

**Confidence:** Clear

### B12. Was there a Mandalore between the Preserver and 3,667 BBY?

**Recommendation:** Don't place Mandalore the Infernal in the 3,950–3,701 BBY span. Record him as an undated Mandalore from "some point before the Great Galactic War" (SWTOR *Onslaught*) in an undated-figures note, with no position in the succession. For this span, keep Gnost-Dural's statement: after Mandalore the Preserver the title went unclaimed for centuries, until Mandalore the Lesser c. 3,667 BBY.

**Why:** The only source for the Infernal is one line in *Onslaught* (2019), which says just "prior to the Great Galactic War" and so could equally mean before 4,000 BBY. Wookieepedia's Mandalore (title)/Legends article doesn't fit him into its succession at all. It goes Ultimate, the unidentified Taung, the Preserver, then "centuries passed without a Mand'alor … until around 3,667 BBY", sourced to SWTOR's *Timeline 3* (2010). Putting the Infernal inside the vacancy would create a contradiction no source requires, while leaving him undated contradicts nothing.

**Sources:** [Mandalore the Infernal](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore_the_Infernal); [Mandalore (title)/Legends §Mandalore the Lesser](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore_%28title%29/Legends); [Mandalore the Lesser](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore_the_Lesser)

**Confidence:** Likely

### B14. Kanz Disorders death toll (3,970–3,670 BBY)

**Recommendation:** Over the whole of the Kanz Disorders, over 500 million Lorrdians and nearly five billion others died, about five and a half billion in all. Write it as "over 500 million Lorrdians and nearly five billion others (some accounts round the total to nearly six billion)".

**Why:** The two figures fit together. The Kanz Disorders/Legends article gives the cited breakdown, sourced to *Han Solo and the Corporate Sector Sourcebook* (1993), *The Essential Chronology* (2000) and *The New Essential Chronology* (2005). Added up, that is above five billion and below six, so both "over five billion" (Galactic Republic/Legends, uncited) and "nearly six billion" fit it. The "nearly six billion" line is a character's spoken estimate in *Star Wars Insider* 139 "Speaking Silently", a story with Clone Captain Rex. Rex comes from TCW, so tag that quote [S-canon] and don't let it set the number.

**Sources:** [Kanz Disorders/Legends §The Disorders](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Kanz_Disorders/Legends) (and lead quote); [Galactic Republic/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Galactic_Republic/Legends); [Lorrdian/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lorrdian/Legends)

**Confidence:** Clear

### B15. What Freia Kallea did in 3,705 BBY

**Recommendation:** Keep both as parts of her first leg (3,705–3,704 BBY). In 3,705 BBY Kallea, in the scoutship *Hope* (secretly funded by Sival Brentioch) and under a commission "as far as Farana", makes her first pushes into space and cuts a path through the Crombach Nebula. By 3,704 BBY she has found a reliable route between Serenno and Telos IV, which links existing lanes, among them the Sprizen End Run and the Mandalorian Road, later sewn into the Hydian Way. Give the route-linking as "3,705–3,704 BBY" rather than pinning it to 3,705.

**Why:** All three versions come from one source, *The Essential Atlas* (2009). The Hydian Way/Legends history section lays out the first leg year by year (3,705 to 3,704 BBY: Crombach Nebula, then Serenno–Telos). The Sprizen End Run and the Mandalorian Road appear only in the article's list of older routes Kallea joined into the Way, without dates. Dating the linking to the whole first leg fits every page without inventing a year.

**Sources:** [Hydian Way/Legends §History](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hydian_Way/Legends); [3705 BBY/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/3705_BBY/Legends); [Freia Kallea/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Freia_Kallea/Legends); [Timeline of galactic history/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Timeline_of_galactic_history/Legends)

**Confidence:** Likely
