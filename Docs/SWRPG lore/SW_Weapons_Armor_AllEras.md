# Weapons and Armour (all Legends eras, focused on the Old Republic and Clone Wars)

Legends lore reference for SWRPG. Researched 21 November 2026. Sources: Wookieepedia (Legends), as cited per entry. Draft for review.

## Summary
This file covers non-lightsaber personal weapons and armour in Legends: blasters and how they work, the history of blaster technology from beam tubes to the modern blaster, ion, disruptor and sonic weapons, slugthrowers, vibroweapons and other melee weapons, armour types from KOTOR-era combat suits to clone trooper and stormtrooper armour, Mandalorian armour, personal energy shields, and anti-Jedi gear (flamethrowers, whipcords, stun nets, sonic weapons, ysalamiri, taozin, phrik electrostaffs and cortosis). It covers all eras but goes deepest on the Old Republic (Great Sith War to the SWTOR era) and the Clone Wars. Lightsabers and the lightsaber-resistant materials themselves (cortosis, phrik, *beskar*) are covered in full in `SW_Lightsabers_Crystals_Materials_AllEras.md`; this file only notes how those materials were used in weapons and armour. The key facts are these:
- A Legends blaster energized a blaster gas (usually Tibanna) with a power pack and fired a focused particle bolt. A gas cartridge typically lasted about 500 shots and a power pack about 100. Blaster technology developed from Rakatan-derived artillery, through beam tubes (c. 25,130 BBY), pulse-wave blasters (7811 BBY) and triple blasters, to the self-contained blaster before the Mandalorian Wars.
- In the KOTOR era, personal energy shields made blasters less decisive, so vibroweapons (often cortosis-woven) and shield-piercing slug and disruptor weapons were common. Personal shields later almost disappeared.
- Anti-Jedi gear worked by getting past the lightsaber: flame, slugs, sonic waves, nets and whipcords that a blade cannot parry, Force-neutral creatures (ysalamiri, taozin), and lightsaber-resistant electrostaffs and armour.

### Blaster (general mechanics)
- **Type:** weapon technology
- **Era:** all eras; mature, self-contained form from before the Mandalorian Wars onward
- **Key facts:**
  - A blaster fired bursts of compressed, high-energy particle beam energy ("bolts") from a replaceable power pack and was the most commonly used weapon in the galaxy. Settings could kill or stun (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) (intro)
  - "Blaster" and "laser" were used interchangeably in everyday speech, but technically the laser was an older design. Blasters recharged faster and fired faster, at the cost of range and accuracy (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("Name")
  - Operation: a small amount of blaster gas (typically Tibanna) passed from the gas cartridge through the Heter valve into the gas conversion enabler chamber (the "XCiter"). There the power pack energized it, the actuating blaster module turned it into a compressed particle beam, and a prismatic crystal or similar device focused it before it left the emitter nozzle. Bolt colour depended on the gas and the focusing device, from red to blue to orange to white (ref: NEGWT; EGW). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("Operation")
  - The gas conversion enabler heated up with firing and could overheat a blaster to the point of destruction. Bolts left a trace of ozone, which Han Solo said smelled like burnt meat (ref: *Legacy of the Force: Tempest*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("Operation")
  - Ammunition: a gas cartridge typically gave up to 500 shots and a power pack up to 100. Weak weapons (e.g. the Defender sporting blaster) used little gas, while disruptors used much more. Older designs used a combined cell/cartridge (ref: EGW). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("Ammunition")
  - Stun setting: power was fed through a secondary emitter that bypassed the gas chamber and produced a ring-shaped electromagnetic burst that disrupted the target's nervous system (ref: EGW). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("Ammunition")
  - Bolts carried no heat themselves. The damage came from the matter they displaced: struck materials deformed and fused, and liquids in organic bodies flashed to steam (ref: EGW). The bolt's passage displacing air made the blaster's sound (ref: *Republic Commando: Hard Contact*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("Ammunition")
  - Accessories included targeting lasers, electronic scopes and larger power packs. Jango Fett's WESTAR-34s had dallorian alloy plating against overheating, and clone DC-15As could interface with clone helmets (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("Blaster accessories")
- **Related:** Tibanna gas; power pack; disruptor; ion blaster; stun.
- **Uncertainty:** The shot counts (500 / 100) are general figures from EGW; individual weapons differ (see the DC-15A and E-11 entries).

### History of blaster technology
- **Type:** technology history
- **Era:** c. 25,130 BBY to the Legacy era
- **Key facts:**
  - The oldest known weapon that could be called a blaster was carried by an ancient droid of the Rakatan Infinite Empire and was considered extremely advanced even compared with blasters of 3956 BBY (ref: KOTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("History and usage")
  - The first blasters of the wider galaxy were reverse-engineered from Rakatan technology after the Infinite Empire fell. They were slow, temperamental heavy artillery. As power sources improved, they shrank into anti-infantry emplacements and then *beam tubes*: a backpack generator with a hose to a handheld focusing tube. Beam tubes armed Xim the Despot's elite Duinarbulon Star Lancers around 25,130 BBY and displaced heatbeams and plasma cannons (ref: EGW). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("History and usage")
  - *Pulse-wave blasters* replaced beam tubes in 7811 BBY, with a more powerful beam and a higher rate of fire. Waymancy Hollow's pulse-wave weapons overpowered the Republic in the Waymancy Storm until the Republic reverse-engineered the Sif-Alulan process (ref: *The Essential Guide to Warfare* Author's Cut, StarWars.com). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("History and usage")
  - The *triple blaster* (three weapons on one targeting system) dated to the early Republic. Pulse-wave weapons were still widely used in the Great Sith War (c. 4000 BBY) but lost out to the triple blaster because they were imprecise and short-ranged (ref: EGW). [conflict] See the pulse-wave entry. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("History and usage")
  - In the lead-up to the Mandalorian Wars, the modern self-contained blaster appeared and triple blasters declined. From then on the blaster was a mature technology: Clone Wars blasters worked the same as Galactic Civil War ones (ref: EGWT). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("History and usage")
  - Clone Wars "stealth" blasters: the DC-19 "Stealth" carbine of clone shadow troopers had a sound suppressor and a stealth mode that used a refined Tibanna mix to make the bolt invisible. The mix had to be reloaded every ten shots, and each shot needed a cool-down (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("History and usage")
  - By the Empire's time, BlasTech was the most common manufacturer and held contracts with both the Empire and the Alliance (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("History and usage")
- **Related:** Pulse-wave blaster; Rakata; Xim the Despot.
- **Uncertainty:** [conflict] about what replaced pulse-wave blasters (see next entry). The exact date of the self-contained blaster is not given [unclear].

### Pulse-wave blaster
- **Type:** ranged weapon (pre-modern blaster)
- **Era:** 7811 BBY to c. 3956 BBY in mainstream use; archaic survivals later
- **Key facts:**
  - Seen as the predecessor of the blaster pistol. Commonly used c. 4000 BBY, but replaced by the first standard blasters by 3956 BBY. By the Rise of the Empire era they were archaic but still used in the Outer Rim and Wild Space (ref: A&E). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Pulse-wave_blaster) (intro)
  - Fired small packets of coherent energy that dissipated with distance, so it was poor at long range. Each shot created a spatial distortion that altered atomic make-up, much like a disruptor, which made even ancient models illegal. Despite this, it was generally weaker than the later blaster (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Pulse-wave_blaster) ("Description")
  - (game stat: cost 200 credits; weight 1 kg; 100 shots per power pack; no inline ref) [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Pulse-wave_blaster) ("Description"); (game stat: maximum range up to 90 m; ref: KOTOR CG) [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Pulse-wave_blaster) (infobox)
  - Krath war droids carried pulse-wave blasters (ref: KOTOR CG). The Charr Ontee soldiers of Kathol used pulse-wave weapons (ref: *Endgame*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Pulse-wave_blaster) ("History")
  - Variants: ATA (Greff-Timms), B-200 Needler, C-202, R-113, U-13; a rifle version, the pulse-wave rifle (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Pulse-wave_blaster) ("Variants")
- **Related:** Blaster history; disruptor; Great Sith War.
- **Uncertainty:** [conflict] What replaced it:
  - A: it was replaced directly by the first standard blasters by 3956 BBY (A&E, 2002).
  - B: it lost out to the triple blaster, and the self-contained blaster came later, before the Mandalorian Wars (EGW, 2012).
  - **Best fit:** B, because it is the newer and more detailed account and explains the triple blaster, which A omits. Both agree that pulse-wave weapons were still in use c. 4000 BBY and gone from mainstream use by the KOTOR era, so the gameplay difference is small. Developer's choice.

### Ion blasters
- **Type:** anti-technology weapon
- **Era:** after the Great Droid Revolution (Old Republic) onward
- **Key facts:**
  - Hand-held weapons that fired ion energy instead of blaster bolts. They disrupted electrical systems, melted fuses and wrecked circuits, and most did little damage to organics. Ion blasters were developed after HK-01's Great Droid Revolution, and the prevalence of assassin and war droids around the Great Sith War led to more advanced models. The Republic used them against the technology-reliant Mandalorians and their droids, and later against Separatist battle droids (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Ion_blaster/Legends) (intro)
  - The Jawa ionization blaster was a stripped blaster with a restraining bolt and an accu-accelerator from an ion drive, causing an ion burst that disabled droids for a short while (game stat: maximum range about 12 m, optimum about 8 m; no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Ion_blaster/Legends) ("Jawa ion blaster")
  - (game stat: ion pistol 250 credits from 32 BBY to 8 ABY; 1 kg; optimal range 8 m; no inline ref) [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Ion_blaster/Legends) ("Pistol")
- **Related:** DC-17 (droid-optimized bolts); Merr-Sonn DEMP guns (Galactic Civil War).
- **Uncertainty:** none noted.

### Disruptors, the Mandalorian ripper and the Mandalorian disintegrator
- **Type:** illegal energy weapons; shield-piercing weapons
- **Era:** Old Republic (KOTOR era) onward
- **Key facts:**
  - A disruptor used many times more blaster gas than a blaster and turned it into a short-ranged, unstable, extremely powerful beam of non-harmonic pulses that broke molecular bonds. It could reduce a humanoid to ash. Drawbacks: few shots, no rapid fire and no stun, except the Tenloss DXR-6 rifle. Some pistols had a single-shot overload that usually ruined the weapon (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Disruptor/Legends) ("Disruptor mechanics")
  - Disruptors were illegal on many planets, sometimes with a death sentence for mere possession. Even under the Empire only a few Imperial Intelligence officers could carry them (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Disruptor/Legends) (intro)
  - Mandalorian ripper: an energy-propelled slugthrower pistol produced in great numbers during the Great Sith War and a common sidearm of Mandalorian officers in the Mandalorian Wars. It ignored personal shielding and, though a slugthrower, was legally classed as a disruptor and banned for civilians on most Republic worlds (no inline ref). A Neo-Crusader commander used one against Revan's party on Kashyyyk in 3961 BBY (ref: KOTOR CG; KOTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_ripper) ("Description", "History")
  - Mandalorian disintegrator: the deadliest disruptor pistol of the Dark Wars. Its white bolts passed through most personal energy shields (ref: KOTOR II), and its use violated the Republic legal code (ref: KOTOR CG; KOTOR II). (game stat: effective range 23 m; ref: KOTOR II) [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_disintegrator) ("Description", "History")
  - Mandalorian heavy repeater: a high-rate frontline weapon of the Mandalorian Wars (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_heavy_repeater) (intro)
- **Related:** Personal energy shields (why shield-piercing mattered); pulse-wave blaster.
- **Uncertainty:** none noted.

### Slugthrowers
- **Type:** projectile firearms
- **Era:** all eras (pre-blaster, then niche)
- **Key facts:**
  - Hand-held projectile weapons that used an explosive chemical to fire a solid slug. Blasters were styled after them. Forms ranged from pistols to rifles (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Slugthrower/Legends) (intro)
  - Blasters displaced slugthrowers as the main infantry weapon because they carried more ammunition, hit harder and used lighter ammunition. Slugthrowers stayed better in very humid places such as jungles, which could ruin a blaster's delicate internals (ref: *Shatterpoint*). They were also easier to silence and showed no visible bolt (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blaster/Legends) ("History and usage")
  - As blasters spread, personal armour fell out of favour, because "even modern armor could not stop a direct blaster hit". The blaster therefore arguably made the slugthrower more useful (ref: *The Star Wars Sourcebook*). [conflict] See the armour entry. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Slugthrower/Legends) ("Description")
  - On Haruun Kal, the Korunnai used slugthrowers before and during the Clone Wars because blasters failed in the jungle (no inline ref). Qymaen jai Sheelal (later Grievous) used a slugthrower rifle in the Huk War (no inline ref). Rebel SpecForce preferred them because they could be silenced, often with explosive ammunition effective against stormtrooper armour (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Slugthrower/Legends) ("History")
  - Czerka Arms was a major manufacturer throughout its history; others were Merr-Sonn and Oriolanis (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Slugthrower/Legends) ("History")
  - (game stat: rifle magazine of 20 slugs, 40 credits, 0.2 kg, some with autofire, maximum up to 450 m; pistol magazine of 10 slugs, 20 credits, 0.1 kg, maximum up to 120 m; ref: Saga Core) [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Slugthrower_rifle/Legends) ("Description"); [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Slugthrower_pistol/Legends) (intro)
  - Slugthrower rifles were used by Tusken Raiders, primitive cultures and petty criminals; models included the Dressellian Projectile Rifle and the Czerka 6-2Aug2 hunting rifle (ref: EotE Core, 2013). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Slugthrower_rifle/Legends) ("Description")
- **Related:** Anti-Jedi gear (slugs cannot be deflected); Mandalorian ripper.
- **Uncertainty:** see [conflict] on armour versus blasters.

### Bowcaster
- **Type:** Wookiee projectile weapon
- **Era:** in use as early as the Jedi Civil War; still used in the Galactic Civil War
- **Key facts:**

### Personal energy shields
- **Type:** defensive technology
- **Era:** Mandalorian Wars onward (prominent in the Jedi Civil War, then nearly lost)
- **Key facts:**
  - A projected energy field against blaster fire, the elements or other hazards, worn on a belt or arm or held like a shield. The standard shield stopped only energy attacks. Mandalorian and Echani variants could block melee attacks, and Arkanian, Verpine and other shields resisted several kinds of attack (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Personal_energy_shield/Legends) (intro)
  - Shields needed lots of power (energy cells or a portable generator), so they were switched on only when combat was expected. They *ablated*, weakening as they absorbed damage, and became very hot (ref: *Dark Forces* and its manual). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Personal_energy_shield/Legends) ("Use")
  - In use as early as the Mandalorian Wars, brought to prominence in the Jedi Civil War, and rumoured to be of Echani origin. Revan's Sith troopers were issued standard energy shields and won bloody battles against Republic troops with ordinary blasters until the Republic copied the technology. Both sides then issued melee weapons such as vibroblades. Shields were usually issued to small elite strike teams and later almost disappeared; Carth Onasi speculated blasters might "catch up" to shields (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Personal_energy_shield/Legends) ("History")
  - By the Battle of Naboo, full-body personal shields existed but produced dangerous radiation and magnetic fields. Gungans used hand-held shields about 1.5 m tall, and Separatist commando droids at the Citadel were shielded (ref: *Star Wars: Imperial Handbook*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Personal_energy_shield/Legends) ("History")
  - From the Galactic Civil War to the Yuuzhan Vong War, personal shields were nearly lost, expensive and limited to exceptional individuals, such as Kyle Katarn (ref: *Dark Forces*; *Jedi Academy*). Stormtrooper commanders of high value could be issued one (ref: *Star Wars: Imperial Handbook*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Personal_energy_shield/Legends) ("History"); [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Stormtrooper_armor/Legends) ("Protective measures")
  - KOTOR-era named shields: Echani shield (energy and sonic) [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Echani_shield); Verpine prototype shield, forearm-mounted, very like an upgraded Arkanian shield, protecting against temperature extremes, sonic and energy weapons [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Verpine_prototype_shield) (both Legends-only pages, no inline ref).
  - Shield gauntlet: a small off-hand energy disc, under a metre across, that could absorb and, with training, deflect blaster fire, even redirecting bolts. Used by the Kilian Rangers and by Bo-Katan's Nite Owls around 20 BBY at Sundari (ref: TCW "The Lawless"). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Shield_gauntlet/Legends)
- **Related:** Vibroweapons; Mandalorian ripper and disintegrator (shield-piercing); Jedi hunter tactics ("lightsabers had a difficult time penetrating most military issue energy shields", no inline ref [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi_hunter/Legends) ("Equipment and tactics")).
- **Uncertainty:** Vibroweapons "cut through energy shields with ease" (Vibroblade page) and the Jedi hunter page says lightsabers had trouble penetrating military shields. These are different weapons, so this is not treated as a conflict [inferred].

The classic design influenced clone, stormtrooper and Royal Guard armour (ref: *Imperial Sourcebook*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_armor/Legends) ("History")
  - Design constants: protection from most blaster fire, with *beskar* armour lightsaber-resistant and able to deflect bolts; a T-visor and HUD; personal decoration. Colour meanings include grey for mourning a lost love, red for honouring a parent, green for duty and blue for reliability (ref: *Legacy of the Force: Revelation*); black for justice and gold for vengeance (ref: *Star Wars Insider* 86); orange for a lust for life (ref: *Imperial Commando: 501st*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_armor/Legends) ("Design")
  - Traditionally twenty pieces: *buy'ce* (helmet), *ghet'bur* (gorget), *hal'cabure* (breastplates), *ka'rta beskar* (tapul / "Iron Heart"), *shar'tas* (placard), *nor'cabur* (backplate), *bes'marbure* (spaulders), *kom'rke* (vambraces), *ven'cabur* (codpiece), *motun'bure* (cuissards), *bes'lovike* (poleyns), *tadun'bure* (greaves) and *cetar'bure* (sabatons), with left/right pairs called *kal* and *beskad* (ref: *Star Wars: The Bounty Hunter Code*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_armor/Legends) ("Armor Pieces")
  - *Beskar* was rare and expensive, so many, including Jango and Boba Fett, used durasteel or duraplast (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_armor/Legends) ("Design")
- **Related:** *Beskar* (see the lightsaber file); Basilisk war droid; flamethrower; wrist rocket; whipcord.
- **Uncertainty:** none noted.

### Clone trooper armour (Clone Wars)
- **Type:** military armour
- **Era:** Clone Wars (Phase II from c. 22 BBY) and the early Empire
- **Key facts:**
  - Made by Kaminoan Armorsmiths Ltd. from plastoid; protected against cold, heat, explosions, physical damage, poison and vacuum (infobox, ref: NEGWT). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Clone_trooper_armor/Legends) (infobox)
  - Phase I: designed with help from Jango Fett, with twenty form-fitting plates of plastoid-alloy composite sealed to a black bodysuit, just under 40 kg, in one size for identical clones, loosely resembling Mandalorian shock trooper armour. Command units first had rank stripes (yellow for commander, red for captain, blue for lieutenant, green for sergeant), and later the colours identified units (ref: *Star Wars: The Bounty Hunter Code*). Kaminoans, who see ultraviolet, saw it as multicoloured (no inline ref). Clones disliked it as heavy and uncomfortable ("body bucket"), blaming the Kaminoans' poor knowledge of human anatomy; it had coolant systems and breathing filters (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Phase_I_clone_trooper_armor/Legends) ("Description")
  - Phase II: introduced around 22 BBY (ref: *Star Wars: Republic* 68) and had largely replaced Phase I by 20 BBY. It added polarized lenses, padding, stronger and lighter plates, better air filtration and oxygen, an annunciator, magnetized boots with a grav-field alternator, and could be pressurized. Colour showed unit, not rank. It was the basis of stormtrooper armour (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Phase_II_clone_trooper_armor/Legends) ("Description")
  - *Katarn*-class commando armour: (game stat: 20 kg; up to 100,000 credits on the black market; no inline ref). Better protection than standard armour, with special plate chemistry and deflector shields. Mark II (about four months after Geonosis) was reinforced against Verpine shatter guns and EMP; Mark III (about ten months after) resisted fire up to laser cannon rounds and light grenades. Fully sealed for 20 minutes in vacuum, with a retractable knuckle vibroblade (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Katarn-class_commando_armor/Legends)
  - ARC trooper armour: lighter and more protective than standard armour, with jetpack, rocket darts, miniature flamethrower and rangefinder options; worn with a blast-dampening *kama* and rank pauldron (ref: *Star Wars Insider* 84, partly; otherwise no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/ARC_trooper_armor/Legends)
- **Related:** DC-15A (helmet interface); Mandalorian armour.
- **Uncertainty:** Phase I has "twenty" plates and stormtrooper armour "eighteen pieces"; these are different suits, so no conflict.

### Stormtrooper armour (Galactic Civil War and later)
- **Type:** military armour
- **Era:** Galactic Empire to 130 ABY and later
- **Key facts:**
  - White plastoid over a sealed black body glove, eighteen pieces, with rank shown by coloured pauldrons (ref: *Star Wars: Imperial Handbook*). Private ownership was forbidden (ref: *Star Wars: The Roleplaying Game*, 2nd ed.). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Stormtrooper_armor/Legends) (intro)
  - It significantly reduced blaster damage but could be fully penetrated by a cannon blast (ref: NEGWT). The breastplate dispersed hand-held energy weapon hits except close-range direct impacts. Forearms could intercept vibroblades (ref: *Imperial Handbook*). It deflected stun beams and greatly reduced shrapnel casualties (ref: NEGWT). It was sealed against chemical and biological attack and could survive vacuum for a limited time (ref: NEGWT), with a 20-minute emergency oxygen tank (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Stormtrooper_armor/Legends) ("Protective measures")
  - Helmet layers: plastoid composite, anti-blaster mesh, magnetic shielding, inner insulator (ref: *Star Wars: The Visual Dictionary*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Stormtrooper_armor/Legends) ("Helmets")
  - By 130 ABY it had reportedly been strengthened to absorb direct blaster hits, though at the Battle of Borosk direct hits still seemed to drop troopers (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Stormtrooper_armor/Legends) ("Protective measures")
- **Related:** Phase II armour; E-11.
- **Uncertainty:** none noted beyond the armour [conflict] above.

### Orbalisk armour
- **Type:** living parasitic "armour"
- **Era:** New Sith Wars / Darth Bane's time (no date on the page)

Lightsabers had a hard time penetrating them, leaving only the exposed neck and wrist joints vulnerable (no inline ref). Only high-voltage electricity was dangerous, though Bane's orbalisks could absorb over a million volts. Dead orbalisks poisoned the host within hours or days (ref: *Darth Bane: Rule of Two*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Orbalisk) (Legends-only page)

### Anti-Jedi gear and tactics (overview)
- **Type:** tactics and equipment
- **Era:** all eras (Hundred-Year Darkness to the Legacy era)
- **Key facts:**
  - Skilled Jedi hunters knew standard tactics usually failed and used unorthodox weapons (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi_hunter/Legends) ("Equipment and tactics")
  - **Slugthrowers:** solid slugs could not be redirected like blaster bolts; they simply vaporized on the blade (ref: *Shatterpoint*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi_hunter/Legends) ("Equipment and tactics")
  - **Flamethrowers:** a jet of fire could not be blocked by a lightsaber. A skilled Jedi could shield themselves with the Force, but only with great effort and uncommon strength (ref: *Star Wars Insider* 85, "A Two-Edged Sword"). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi_hunter/Legends) ("Equipment and tactics"); [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Flamethrower/Legends) ("Description")
  - **Sonic weapons** interfered with a Jedi's concentration (ref: *Revenge of the Sith* novelization), and sonic bolts flowed around the blade (Sonic weapon entry above). HK-47 rated sonic weapons and high explosives best, with poison gas effective to a lesser degree; quality poisoned darts were silent and too fast to counter (ref: KOTOR II). Land mines worked well because most Jedi fought at close range (no inline ref). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi_hunter/Legends) ("Equipment and tactics")
  - **Lightsaber-resistant gear:** the 1,001st stormtrooper division and IG-100 MagnaGuards used phrik electrostaffs. Cad Bane had a handmade carbine firing cortosis projectiles that could short out lightsaber blades; the ammunition was expensive and stressed the weapon, so he rarely used it (ref: *Star Wars Fact File* 2013, "Cad Bane's Equipment"; *The Clone Wars: Act on Instinct*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi_hunter/Legends) ("Equipment and tactics")
  - **Revan's techniques** for non-Force-users (KOTOR II): target a Jedi's allies or bystanders, snipe from cover (many powers need line of sight), don't over-plan (Jedi precognition), mask the mind by broadcasting strong unrelated emotions, and use psychological warfare (ref: KOTOR II). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi_hunter/Legends) ("Equipment and tactics")
  - Cybernetic hunters (Grievous) and droids, which were hard to sense in the Force, were effective; some creatures were bred to hunt Jedi, such as voxyn and terentatek (ref: *Labyrinth of Evil*; KOTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi_hunter/Legends) ("Overview")
- **Related:** All entries below.
- **Uncertainty:** none noted.

## Game notes (optional, suggestions only)
- *Suggestion:* For KOTOR-era campaigns, personal energy shields make a good reason for melee-heavy fights; shield-piercing weapons (Mandalorian ripper, disintegrator, disruptors) can be rare, illegal and much-wanted.
- *Suggestion:* Use the cortosis purity split from the conflict note: "cortosis-weave" gear only resists, while rare refined cortosis shields or gauntlets short out a blade. This gives a ladder of anti-Jedi loot.
- *Suggestion:* Non-Force-user enemies hunting Jedi can follow Revan's and HK-47's doctrine: flame, slugs, sonic weapons, mines, nets and attacks on companions, rather than duels.
- *Suggestion:* For Clone Wars settings, MagnaGuard-style phrik electrostaffs and Separatist cost-cutting ("many, though not all" were phrik) allow cheap non-phrik staffs that lightsabers *can* cut.
