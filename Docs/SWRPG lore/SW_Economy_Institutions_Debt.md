## Summary
- **Taxes and tariffs.**
  - Republic: taxes from working beings, collected by the Republic Tax Collection Agency, and a 15% transit tax late in its life. Its attempt to tax Outer Rim trade routes (33 BBY) triggered the Naboo blockade.
  - Empire: taxed through the Imperial Taxation Bureau, the CSA's 3%/9%/20% stipend and the Hutt Council. Its main interest in spice was collecting tax on it.
  - Local tariffs: run from 50% (Torina) and 100% (Shalam) up to a 500% minimum (game stat).
- **Banking.** Banking is dominated by the IGBC (Muunilinst, Aargau, Scipio, Mygeeto) and by the Bank of the Core, which mints most CSA currency and holds over three-quarters of CSA loans.
- **Debt and interest.** Recorded rates: the IGBC at 25% (Clone Wars), a Mos Eisley bank at 15% against loan sharks at 300%+ (game stat). Collectors range from Hailfire droids and Grievous to Davik Kang's hunters, who were sent over a 100 cr missed repayment (3956 BBY).
- **Crime cartels.** The Hutts and the Exchange take tribute in fixed sums, e.g. 600 cr on Nar Shaddaa (3951 BBY) and 9,000 cr a year from Onacra (3 BBY–0 ABY).

### Republic taxation and trade law
- **Type:** Taxes and tariffs.
- **Era:** Old Republic to 19 BBY.

**Key facts:**
- [in-universe] Late Republic, galaxy-wide: working beings paid taxes, collected by the Republic Tax Collection Agency. Source: W:Galactic_Republic/Legends (§Economy).
- [in-universe] "In the later years" of the Republic, a 15% transit tax was levied for revenue. Source: W:Galactic_Republic/Legends (§Economy).
- [in-universe] 124 BBY, Outer Rim: the Senate declared the whole Outer Rim a Free Trade Zone. In practice this exempted the Trade Federation from local taxation, and other sectors became its perpetual customers. Source: W:Trade_Federation/Legends (§Cartelization; *Essential Guide to Warfare*, 2012).
- [in-universe] 67 BBY: in a deal arranged by Hego Damask, the Trade Federation got a Senate seat with full voting rights and accepted reform of the Free Trade Zones. This cost it "some of their shipping monopoly". Source: W:Trade_Federation/Legends (*Darth Plagueis*, 2012).
- [in-universe] 33 BBY (taxation of trade routes):
  - After the Eriadu incident, the Republic passed taxation of trade routes to outlying systems, both to weaken the Federation and to "recoup funds for the ailing Republic".
  - The Federation allied with the IGBC, the Corporate Alliance and the Techno Union, which were also hit by the taxation.
  - The Senate drafted Resolution BR-0371; Lott Dod filibustered.
  - Sources: W:Invasion_of_Naboo/Legends (§The taxation of trade routes); W:Trade_Federation/Legends (§Terrorist attacks).
- [in-universe] 32 BBY, Naboo: the Blockade of Naboo protested the "new shipping-lane taxes". Gunray was prepared to let Naboo starve unless the regulations were removed. Source: W:Invasion_of_Naboo/Legends (§The Blockade of Naboo).
- [in-universe] 33 BBY: the Republic Customs Office inspected Federation cargo. The droid army was declared as expensive binary loadlifters. Source: W:Trade_Federation/Legends (*Complete Locations*, 2005).
- [in-universe] 24 BBY, Korriban: the Commerce Guild opened an office on the "tax-free world". Source: W:Commerce_Guild/Legends (§History).
- [in-universe] 22 BBY, Chandrila: under the Military Creation Act, local tax revenues earmarked for local forces were funnelled to the Army of the Republic, and 575 Public Safety Commission staff walked out. Source: W:Military_Creation_Act/Legends.
- [in-universe] 3956 BBY, Ahto City, Manaan: the Civil Authority fined both the Sith Empire and the Republic 10,000 cr each. Source: W:Unidentified_Sith_diplomat (KOTOR, 2003).

**Related:** Trade Federation; SW_Economy_Events.

**Uncertainty:** No rate is given for the Republic income tax.

### Imperial taxation and penalties
- **Type:** Taxes and fines.
- **Era:** 19 BBY–4 ABY (and Remnant).

**Key facts:**
- [game stat: Han Solo and the Corporate Sector Sourcebook, WEG 1993] Empire–CSA arrangement:
  - The CSA paid the Empire a yearly stipend of 3% of total gross product, 9% of all material and 20% of all strategic rare elements.
  - Sources: W:Galactic_Empire/Legends (§Economy); W:Corporate_Sector_Authority/Legends (§Restructuring: 3% of gross product plus "a tithe of materials and ore produced").
- [in-universe] Imperial era: the Empire's main concern with spice was "to collect taxes from its shipment and distribution through legitimate channels", not to stop it. Source: W:Smuggler/Legends (§Smuggled goods).
- [in-universe] Imperial era: the Hutt Council "arranged for considerable taxes to support the greatly expanded Imperial Military". Source: W:Hutt_Space/Legends.
- [game stat: The Far Orbit Project, WEG 1998] 0 ABY: the Imperial Taxation Bureau's Star Galleon *Emperor's Will* carried about 400 million cr of taxes from primitive Outer Rim worlds, in precious metals, minerals, spices and artwork. Source: W:Emperor's_Will_(Star_Galleon-class).
- [game stat: Shadows of the Empire Planets Guide, WEG 1997] Spice offences under the Imperial Penal Code:
  - Smuggling spice (buying without a receipt or moving it without a permit) was a class four violation: a month in prison and a fine of up to 5,000 cr.
  - Breaking local spice customs was class five: a fine of 100–5,000 cr.
  - Source: W:Spice/Legends (§History).
- [game stat: Platt's Starport Guide, WEG 1995] Holoseal tampering was a Class Four infraction: a fine of 1,000–5,000 cr, a month in prison and confiscation of the cargo. Sources: W:Holoseal; W:Imperial_Office_of_Customs/Legends.
- [in-universe] 0 BBY: critics said Imperial control of the economy appeared to be "dramatically expanding". The Empire nationalized some corporations, mainly CIS supporters and later Rebel sympathizers. Source: W:Galactic_Empire/Legends (§Economy).

### Local taxes, tariffs and fees (by planet)
- **Type:** Local fiscal rules.
- **Era:** Various.

**Key facts:**
- [game stat: Shadows of the Empire Planets Guide, WEG 1997] Bothawui, Galactic Civil War: annual operating taxes (covering rent, utilities and permits) ran from 8,000 cr for small businesses to over 250,000 cr for large factories. A weapons permit cost 3,000 cr and took three weeks. Source: W:Bothawui/Legends (§Laws).
- [game stat: Planets of the Galaxy, Volume Three, WEG 1993] Torina, Imperial era: a 50% tariff on imported electronics protected local industry, and local consumer electronics sold for 20% below normal rates. Source: W:Torina.
- [in-universe] Shalam, Galactic Civil War era [unclear year]: a 100% tariff on imported jandarra forced Jubilar's people to smuggle it in. Sources: W:Jandarra; W:Shalam ("The Last One Standing", *Tales of the Bounty Hunters*, 1996).
- [game stat: The Politics of Contraband, WEG 1992] Planetary Tariff Code, Alternative Minimum Tax: native-grown protected or subsidized plants could not be imported without a minimum tax of "no less than 500%" of current retail value. Source: W:Planetary_Tariff_Code.
- [in-universe] c.4–3 BBY, Dilonexa XXIII: a 1,350 cr message import fee, with a 2,000 cr penalty. Source: W:Dilonexa_XXIII (*Lando Calrissian and the Flamewind of Oseon*, 1983).
- [unclear tier] Starship captain's licence: a background check, ten years as a spacer and a 300 cr fee, or 500 cr to expedite. Source: W:Captain/Legends. The page's sources include WEG RPG books; the year is not given.
- [in-universe] c.40 ABY: the Galactic Alliance demanded that members pay taxes for reconstruction and pool their militaries. Source: W:Galactic_Federation_of_Free_Alliances (§Second Galactic Civil War).

### Aargau and the Three Statutes
- **Type:** Banking world law.
- **Era:** Discovered 20,000–17,000 BBY; active to the New Republic.

**Key facts:**
- [in-universe] Aargau's government was the Bank of Aargau, owned by the IGBC. It had only three laws, all carrying the death penalty:
  - No export of rare metals without approval.
  - No weapons for non-Aargauuns, and compulsory arms for citizens.
  - No "defraud, discredit or deceive" against the Bank.
  - Source: W:Aargau/Legends (§Description).
- [in-universe] Imperial era: Bank of Aargau Security was the largest privately owned army in the galaxy. BAS Customs monitored all traffic. Source: W:Aargau/Legends (Wizards web article "Aargau: For All Your Banking Needs", 2003).
- [in-universe] By 3963 BBY Aargau was a major financial centre. It competed with the Telerath Interstellar Banking Initiative until Telerath was abandoned during the Mandalorian Wars. Source: W:Aargau/Legends (§Early history; *Knights of the Old Republic* 11).
- [in-universe] 22 BBY: Senator Aks Moe visited to negotiate IGBC funding for a Republic military initiative and was assassinated. Source: W:Aargau/Legends (HNN 50).
- [in-universe] Clone Wars: the Republic garrisoned clones on Aargau to protect its "substantial investments". Source: W:Aargau/Legends.
- [in-universe] 3 ABY:
  - Leia sought an Alliance loan for X-wings, with the Crown Jewels of Alderaan as collateral.
  - Vader stole the jewels once the loan was approved, and both were tried in absentia.
  - Source: W:Aargau/Legends (*Star Wars* (1977) 48, Marvel).
- [in-universe] GCW: Vader held an account on Aargau. Lando hacked it to plant Treece's embezzled funds. Source: W:Aargau/Legends (*Star Wars* (1977) 57).

### The InterGalactic Banking Clan (IGBC) and Muun finance
- **Type:** Banking guild.
- **Era:** From the founding of the Republic to the Legacy era.

**Key facts:**
- [in-universe] Founding and early role:
  - It dates from the creation of the Republic and dictated wealth flows between the Core and the Outer Rim.
  - It funded governments, settlements, guilds, trade corporations and shipping cartels.
  - Source: W:InterGalactic_Banking_Clan/Legends (§Origin; *Darth Plagueis*).
- [in-universe] Structure: a union of Muunilinst's Council of Banking Clans and other banking powers. Subsidiaries included the Bank of Aargau, the Muunilinst Banking Clan, the InterGalactic Bank of Kuat, the Dressian Kiolsh Merchant Bank and Bank Niro Eleven. Source: W:InterGalactic_Banking_Clan/Legends.
- [in-universe] Debt enforcement:
  - The Collections and Security Division used Iotran soldiers and Hailfire droids.
  - In the late Republic the IGBC bought thousands of Hailfire droids "to discourage customers from defaulting on their loans".
  - It hired mercenaries to collect payments and debts.
  - Sources: W:InterGalactic_Banking_Clan/Legends (§Organization); W:Muun/Legends (§Cornering the market; §Society and culture, *New Essential Guide to Alien Species*).
- [in-universe] c.3643 BBY:
  - A companion recovered the IGBC's original articles of incorporation.
  - A heist on an IGBC vault took place (SWTOR crew skills).
  - Earlier, Andronikos Revel, then an accountant on Muunilinst, robbed the IGBC head of "a few million credits".
  - Source: W:InterGalactic_Banking_Clan/Legends (§Origin).
- [in-universe] c.1000 BBY: Lord Qordis embezzled Brotherhood of Darkness funds into an anonymous IGBC account. Source: W:InterGalactic_Banking_Clan/Legends (*Darth Bane: Rule of Two*).
- [in-universe] 62 BBY: Naboo planned to restructure its debt with the Banking Clan, for tax cuts and lower interest rates. Source: W:Invasion_of_Naboo/Legends (§Naboo in the galactic community).
- [in-universe] Separatist Crisis: Palpatine opened secret accounts that diverted "billions of credits" from several planets, tax-free, to IGBC-linked corporations. Source: W:InterGalactic_Banking_Clan/Legends (§Separatist Crisis).
- [in-universe] Clone Wars, debts and seizures:
  - Phlut Design Systems defaulted on an IGBC capital loan; the IGBC seized its assets, and its IG lancer droids went to the CIS.
  - The IGBC funded Grievous's reconstruction in return for his service as a collections agent.
  - Source: W:InterGalactic_Banking_Clan/Legends (§Clone Wars).
- [in-universe] Clone Wars, Senate and the Clovis affair:
  - The Senate passed legislation deregulating the Banking Clan to open new credit lines for the military.
  - Dooku had Clovis levy debts on the Republic; the Republic took Scipio.
  - The banks were placed under Palpatine's control (effectively nationalized).
  - Source: W:InterGalactic_Banking_Clan/Legends (§Clone Wars; TCW "Heroes on Both Sides", "The Rise of Clovis").
- [in-universe] Clone Wars (c.20 BBY), Coruscant: the Banking Clan's 25% interest charges drained Republic social programmes. Source: W:Bail_Prestor_Organa/Legends (TCW "Heroes on Both Sides").
- [in-universe] Imperial era: Imperial monitors sat in Muunilinst's institutions and harsh decrees hampered Muun lending. Source: W:Muunilinst/Legends (§Galactic Empire).
- [in-universe] New Republic era: the IGBC was creditor to both the New Republic and the Remnant. Source: W:InterGalactic_Banking_Clan/Legends (§Imperial Period).
- [in-universe] c.20 BBY: Clan Besadii secretly funnelled casino profits to the Separatists. Source: W:Besadii/Legends (TCW comic "The Professional!").

**Related:** SW_Economy_Currencies; SW_Economy_Events (the 22 BBY currency boom; the Battle of Muunilinst).

### Other banks and financial institutions

- **Era:** Various.

  - At the height of the New Order it held majority interests in over 3,000 banks.

### Corporations and trade guilds
- **Type:** Commercial powers.
- **Era:** Various.

**Key facts:**
- [in-universe] Czerka (Old Sith Wars):
  - At its zenith, with influence over half the galaxy's worlds, Czerka was "considered to be a monopoly".
  - It traded with the Hutt Cartel and independent worlds "with impunity".
  - It was rumoured to have let colonists starve when its high prices were not met.
  - Source: W:Czerka_Arms/Legends.

### The Exchange and Hutt cartels (additions only)
- **Type:** Criminal syndicates.
- **Era:** Old Republic to New Republic.

**Key facts:**
- [in-universe] c.4000–3956 BBY, Taris: the Exchange was based there, and the Taris Civil Authority "only accepted bribes" to keep quiet or collaborate. Source: W:Taris/Legends (§Swoop racing and crime).
- [in-universe] 3951 BBY, galaxy-wide: the Exchange's smuggling ring "developed unofficial profits for entire sectors" and propped up poor Republic worlds. Source: W:Exchange (§First Jedi Purge; KOTOR II).
- [in-universe] Nar Shaddaa, Hutt district vassals: vassals collected fees and tolls and passed a share up to their Hutt overlords. Source: W:Nar_Shaddaa/Legends. This repeats the devfile; cross-reference SW_NarShaddaa_HuttSpace_GGW_Part1.
- [in-universe] Galactic Civil War: the Hutt Cartel held "a monopoly of smuggler employment". Because the Hutts mattered to the Outer Rim economy, any Imperial move against the smuggler trade needed High Command clearance. Source: W:Hutt_Cartel/Legends (*Imperial Handbook*, 2014).
- [in-universe] Clone Wars: the Hutts sided with the Republic, and economic benefits encouraged compromise. Source: W:Hutt_Space/Legends (§Politics).
- [in-universe] Late 32 BBY, Tatooine: with Gardulla removed, Jabba had "a virtual monopoly" on Tatooine's underworld. Source: W:Tatooine/Legends (*Star Wars: Bounty Hunter*).

### Smuggling economics and black markets
- **Type:** Illegal trade.
- **Era:** Various.

**Key facts:**
- [in-universe] A smuggler (euphemism: "free trader") moved goods or people covertly "to prevent taxation or some other impediment". Source: W:Smuggler/Legends.
- [game stat: Galaxy Guide 6: Tramp Freighters, WEG 1990] Imperial-era black market:
  - Goods were sold there even when legal, to avoid taxes or notifying authorities.
  - Availability codes:
    - F (Fee): a fee or permit of 5–50% of the item's cost, averaging 10%.
    - R (Restricted): a licence, a background check and a fee of 100% of cost or more.
  - The black market avoided both the fees and the ownership records.
  - Source: W:Black_market/Legends.
- [in-universe] c.21 BBY, Sundari: Moogan smugglers imported slabin-laced tea into Sundari as long as the customs officer was "sufficiently bribed". Sources: W:Mandalorian/Legends (§New Mandalorian corruption); W:Credit_ingot/Legends.
- [in-universe] 8.5 ABY, Tatooine: Chandrilan brandy was used to bribe port officials; a bottle cost 2,000 cr. Source: W:Chandrilan_brandy/Legends (*Tatooine Ghost*).
- [in-universe] Galactic Civil War: many smugglers channelled funds to the Rebellion. Source: W:Smuggler/Legends (§History).
- [in-universe] 28 ABY, Coruscant: smugglers persuaded senators they had bribed to back Cal Omas. Source: W:Yuuzhan_Vong_War (§The election of 28 ABY).
- [in-universe] Clone Wars, Mandalore: the New Mandalorian "corruption" arc shows customs bribery as the cost of neutrality. Source: W:Mandalorian/Legends.

**Related:** SW_Economy_Prices_Goods (spice and contraband prices).

### Slavery as an economic system (historical fact only)
- **Type:** Slave trade.
- **Era:** Various.

**Key facts:**
- [in-universe] c.3.5 ABY: Vigo Lonay reported a 53% drop in slave revenues in his sector after several planets joined the Alliance and outlawed slavery. Source: W:Slavery/Legends (*Shadows of the Empire*, 1996).
- [in-universe] Imperial era: Imperial institutions preferred Wookiee and other slaves for projects like the first Death Star, "even though the droids were economically cheaper". Source: W:Galactic_Empire/Legends (§Xenophobia).
- [in-universe] Clone Wars era: the Zygerrian Slave Empire abducted primitive beings for profit. Source: W:Zygerrian_Slave_Empire/Legends.

### Bacta and kolto trades (additions only)
- **Type:** Medical commodity trade.
- **Era:** Old Republic to New Republic.
- [in-universe] Cross-reference SW_Kolto_Bacta for the kolto monopoly, the price rise and the switch to bacta.
- [in-universe] Bacta Cartel: a monopoly of Zaltin and Xucphra, the two bacta producers, "largely responsible for inflating bacta prices". Source: W:Bacta_Cartel/Legends.
- [in-universe] 44 BBY, Stark Hyperspace War:
  - Amid a bacta shortage, Iaco Stark raided Trade Federation bacta shipments and sold them cheaply.

  - Sources: W:Trade_Federation/Legends (§The Stark Hyperspace War); W:Stark_Commercial_Combine.
