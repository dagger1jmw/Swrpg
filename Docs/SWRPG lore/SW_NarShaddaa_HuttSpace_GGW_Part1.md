# Nar Shaddaa and Hutt Space in the Great Galactic War (c. 3681–3653 BBY)

Legends lore reference for SWRPG. Researched Nov 21, 2026. Sources: Wookieepedia (Legends), as cited per entry. Draft for review.

## Summary
This file covers Nar Shaddaa and the rest of Hutt Space as a home base for a smuggler, bounty hunter, criminal, refugee or neutral character during the Great Galactic War (3681–3653 BBY). It focuses on 3661–3660 BBY, when Hylo Visz gathered her blockade-runners on the moon.

The sources say:
- The Hutt Cartel stayed neutral through the whole war. It resented the Emperor for leaving it out of his plans, but Imperial agents blocked every Republic attempt to win it over, and a Hutt–Imperial treaty made the neutrality formal.
- Hutt money still reached both sides. Hutts funded Visz's blockade run (the Hydian Way; The SIS page calls the route the "Rimma Trade Route"; recorded as an error or variant, not a second route (ruling 11 (lore batch 17))) against the Empire's Mandalorian allies, a Hutt sold prototype drives to a Republic firm, and in 3653 BBY Hutt crime lords tipped off the Sith blockade at Coruscant.
- Nar Shaddaa itself is thinly described for these years. The Visz story gives an Undercity, Hutt-owned shipyards and docks, gangs of orphans and an unnamed cantina. Most named districts and landmarks are attested only for the Cold War (post-window) or for older eras, and are flagged as such.

### A. Nar Shaddaa: the moon, its rule, and what is attested when
- **Type:** location
- **Era:** general description; dated items tagged
- **Key facts:**
  - **The moon (general, not era-specific):**
    - The largest moon of Nal Hutta, in the Y'Toub system on the Mid Rim/Outer Rim border [Nar Shaddaa/Legends §Overview](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa/Legends).
    - An ecumenopolis also called the Vertical City, the Smugglers' Moon, "Little Coruscant" and "Narsh". It was the Hutt Cartel's main spaceport for Nal Hutta [Nar Shaddaa/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa/Legends); [Nar Shaddaa/Legends §Overview](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa/Legends).
    - Roughly 100 city levels; the lower half is the Undercity, lit only artificially [Nar Shaddaa/Legends §Surface](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa/Legends).
    - Unlike Coruscant, both the upper and lower levels were dangerous. Anyone could build gantries, bridges and signs, and some signs deliberately led victims into ambushes [Nar Shaddaa/Legends §Surface](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa/Legends).
  - **History:**
    - The Hutts made the displaced Evocii build the city. Survivors mutated in the Undercity [Nar Shaddaa/Legends §Old Republic](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa/Legends); [Evocii §Refuge on Nar Shaddaa](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Evocii).
    - When the Ootmian Pabol route was cut in 4000 BBY, legitimate trade left and the moon became the galaxy's notorious "shadowport" [Hutt/Legends §The Old Republic](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hutt/Legends); [Hutt Space/Legends §Republic Entanglements](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hutt_Space/Legends).
  - **Rule (general, not dated):**
    - No capital or central government. Each sector was run by one Hutt crime lord tied to a kajidic on Nal Hutta. Districts were held by vassals, "mostly Exchange related thugs", who collected tolls and passed a cut upward [Nar Shaddaa/Legends §Government and law enforcement](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa/Legends).
    - The Grand Hutt Council on Nal Hutta "rarely interfered" [Nar Shaddaa/Legends §Government and law enforcement](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa/Legends).
    - No courts: disputes were settled by bribing or influencing the Hutts and their vassals [Nar Shaddaa/Legends §Government and law enforcement](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa/Legends).
    - No police: residents hired bounty hunters or mercenaries, or formed neighbourhood militias [Nar Shaddaa/Legends §Government and law enforcement](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa/Legends).
  - **Economy (general):** "Anything illegal elsewhere" was for sale. Many young smugglers and criminals began their careers here, and corporations tested dangerous technology in the lower levels to dodge regulation [Nar Shaddaa/Legends §Economy](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa/Legends).
  - **Attested in the window (3681–3653 BBY):**
    - **Undercity alleys:** home to Visz's gang of orphaned alien children after her refugee transport left her behind, early in the war [Hylo Visz §Early life](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hylo_Visz).
    - **Hutt Cartel cargo docks and shipyards in the Undercity:** Visz worked there as a mechanic for years [Hylo Visz §Early life](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hylo_Visz).

    - **Barrga the Hutt's base:** Barrga was "based out of" Nar Shaddaa [Barrga](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Barrga). The ion drives he sold were developed on the moon [Rendili Vehicle Corporation/Legends §History](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Rendili_Vehicle_Corporation/Legends).

### B. The Hutt Cartel in the war
- **Type:** organisation
- **Era:** 3681–3653 BBY; aftermath post-window
- **Key facts:**
  - **Structure (general description, not era-specific):**
    - Kajidics (Hutt clan-businesses) competed under the Grand Hutt Council. Each had a leader called a kajidii or lorda [Kajidic/Legends §Hutt Kajidics](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Kajidic/Legends).
    - The overall head was the Supreme Mogul [Supreme Mogul](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Supreme_Mogul).
  - **Leader during the window:**
    - Karagga the Unyielding was Supreme Mogul "for nearly two centuries by the Cold War era" and ruled from a palace on Nal Hutta [Karagga §Biography](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Karagga). That puts him in office throughout 3681–3653 BBY [inferred from the count].
    - His rise began when he wiped out an Exchange cell at a peace meeting; the date is not given [Karagga §Biography](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Karagga).
    - His successor Toborro took over only after Karagga's death in the Cold War (post-window) [Toborro §Biography](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Toborro).
  - **Neutrality:**
    - The Cartel was "displeased" that the Emperor left it out of his plans. Imperial Intelligence agents nonetheless kept the Republic from winning its support, and it stayed neutral like most of the underworld [Hutt Cartel/Legends §Old Republic](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hutt_Cartel/Legends); [Imperial Intelligence (Sith Empire) §History](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Imperial_Intelligence_%28Sith_Empire%29).
    - Hutt Space would have sided with the Republic but for "the hard fought actions of Imperial Intelligence" [Hutt Space/Legends §Politics](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hutt_Space/Legends).
    - In 3667 BBY the Cartel was still "debating" which side to back. Imperial Diplomatic Service courting did little to change that [Blockade of the Hydian Way §The Sith in reflection](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blockade_of_the_Hydian_Way).
    - Nal Hutta stayed "exactly as they liked it" [Nal Hutta/Legends §Old Republic](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nal_Hutta/Legends).
  - **The Hutt–Imperial treaty:**
    - Brokered by the Ministry of Intelligence. The Hutts kept their sovereignty and neutrality provided that no clan or subsidiary "worked to further the interests" of the Republic [Hutt–Imperial treaty](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hutt%E2%80%93Imperial_treaty); [Hutt–Imperial treaty §Terms](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hutt%E2%80%93Imperial_treaty).
    - If it was broken, the Sith could force the Cartel to name the violators as traitors [Hutt–Imperial treaty §Terms](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hutt%E2%80%93Imperial_treaty).
    - It was "widely obeyed" through the war [Hutt–Imperial treaty §Reception](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hutt%E2%80%93Imperial_treaty). The first known breach is the three Quesh families, c. 3643 BBY (post-window) [Hutt–Imperial treaty §History](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hutt%E2%80%93Imperial_treaty); [Quesh §Geographical features](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Quesh).
    - See the conflict on Quesh's timing below.
  - **Playing both sides within the window:**
    - **Against the Empire's allies:** the Cartel bankrolled Visz's blockade run "provided that they receive a considerable return" [Hylo Visz §Breaking the blockade](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hylo_Visz); [Blockade of the Hydian Way §Smugglers take the lead](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blockade_of_the_Hydian_Way). About a year later it came to collect. Visz had lost her pay to Lokai, so she went into hiding [Hylo Visz §Going into hiding](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hylo_Visz).
    - **Selling to the Republic:** Barrga sent experimental ion drives, developed on Nar Shaddaa, to the Rendili Vehicle Corporation on Corellia. Visz was told they were "for usage in military wares". Rendili double-crossed him, blowing up the couriers along with Visz's *Crimson Fleece* [Hylo Visz §The debacle on Corellia](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hylo_Visz); [Rendili Vehicle Corporation/Legends §History](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Rendili_Vehicle_Corporation/Legends); [Musk (Nikto)](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Musk_%28Nikto%29). The date is a [conflict] (Decision 2).
    - **Helping the Sith:** in 3653 BBY "the Hutt crime lords", wanting into the spice trade, hired the bounty hunter Vrath Xizor to stop Korr's engspice drop. Xizor warned Darth Malgus's blockade [Zeerid Korr §Mission for the Exchange](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Zeerid_Korr); [Sacking of Coruscant §Zeerid Korr](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sacking_of_Coruscant).
  - **What it traded (window):** black-market goods, contraband "and people", run by Cartel smugglers like Visz [Hylo Visz §Working for the Cartel](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hylo_Visz); prototype ion drives [Barrga](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Barrga); spice ambitions [Zeerid Korr §Mission for the Exchange](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Zeerid_Korr).
  - **Feuds within the window:**
    - Nok Drayen was credited with killing "half the members of the Hutt Cartel in a single night" during the Great Galactic War. The Cartel then gave him "an entire quadrant of the galaxy" as a peace offering [Nok Drayen §Rise to power](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nok_Drayen). Exact date not given [unclear].
    - No other Cartel war is dated to 3681–3653 BBY.
  - **Hutts attested as alive in the window:**
    - Karagga [inferred].
    - Barrga [Barrga](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Barrga).
    - Suudaa Nem'ro, who "lived during the Great Galactic War". He took over the town of Jiguuna on Nal Hutta about two centuries before the Cold War [Suudaa Nem'ro](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Suudaa_Nem%27ro); [Jiguuna §History](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jiguuna).
    - Voontara Fa'athra, born after 3843 BBY; his named actions are post-window [Voontara Fa'athra §Biography](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Voontara_Fa%27athra).
  - **Hutt attitudes to Jedi (general):** Hutts disliked Jedi, who followed criminals into Hutt Space. Jedi usually kept quiet or announced themselves to the local Hutt first [Hutt Space/Legends §Politics](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hutt_Space/Legends).

### C. The Exchange and other syndicates
- **Type:** organisations
- **Era:** as tagged
- **Key facts:**
  - **Exchange structure** (described for the old Exchange; [other era] for detail):
    - Led by the "Compeer". Below were crime lords who controlled profitable worlds, admitted only by nomination [Exchange §Organization](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Exchange).
    - Planetary cells were run by bosses, who reported to secret sector chiefs [Exchange §Organization](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Exchange).
    - Trades: spice refinement and smuggling, extortion, weapons trafficking, slave trading and bounty hunting [Exchange](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Exchange).
    - The old Exchange collapsed and a new one replaced it. "Around the time of the Galactic Wars" it was based out of Nar Shaddaa, chiefly active in the Cold War [Exchange §Organization](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Exchange).
  - **In the window:**
    - Zeerid Korr ran weapons for the Exchange "all over the Outer Rim" under the handler Oren, fell into debt, and was moved onto spice runs [Zeerid Korr §Soldier then smuggler](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Zeerid_Korr); [Zeerid Korr §Mission for the Exchange](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Zeerid_Korr).
    - Engspice was "especially addictive" (users could only be satisfied by the same spice) and "incredibly expensive" [Engspice](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Engspice).
    - Mos Ila on Tatooine was founded by Jawas "in the years of the Great Galactic War", then taken over by the Exchange. Later the Empire arrived and deposed it; the date is not given [Mos Ila](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mos_Ila).
    - No Exchange boss is named for 3681–3653 BBY.
  - **Exchange vs Hutts:**
    - In the Cold War (post-window) they were "major rival[s]"; Ukabi was the Exchange boss on Nar Shaddaa [Exchange §Cold War](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Exchange).
    - Ukabi's defeat by the Shadow Syndicate is dated two ways [conflict] (Decision 3).
    - Karagga's undated destruction of an Exchange cell (entry B).
  - **Shadow Syndicate** (Godoba the Hutt, Vaarko Tiyai of the Nar Shaddaa Labor Alliance, DX-TX): an Imperial-aligned bloc assembled by Maro Vizhen of the Imperial Diplomatic Service. Attested "by 3643 BBY" (post-window) [Shadow Syndicate/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Shadow_Syndicate/Legends); [Shadow Syndicate/Legends §Ukabi incident](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Shadow_Syndicate/Legends).
  - **Nok Drayen's syndicate:**
    - Founded during the war from the loot of the Mandellia shadowport, whose residents Drayen killed with a nerve-toxin torpedo stolen from the Imperial Navy [Nok Drayen §Rise to power](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nok_Drayen).
    - He fought the Rath Cartel and Vandelhelm Combine "during the Syndicate Wars" (undated) [Nok Drayen §Rise to power](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nok_Drayen).
    - In 3653 BBY he chaired the Star Cluster Casino meeting, executed Naatu for informing to the Republic, and introduced Darth Angral [Nok Drayen §Cold War](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nok_Drayen); [Naatu](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Naatu).
  - **Migrant Merchants' Guild:** see the Coruscant file [Migrant Merchants' Guild](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Migrant_Merchants%27_Guild).

### D. Smugglers and spacers
- **Type:** occupation / people
- **Era:** 3681–3653 BBY
- **Key facts:**
  - **How wartime smuggling worked:**
    - The Republic often offered contracts to smugglers. The Empire, however, had "pressured most legitimate pilots" into refusing Republic runs [Hylo Visz §Working for the Cartel](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hylo_Visz).
    - Visz took a Sith contract early on and swore off the Empire, because it "cared very little for the well-being of freelancers" [Hylo Visz §Early life](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hylo_Visz).

She gathered Zale Barrows, Tyresius Lokai and Lokai's friend Quan on Nar Shaddaa, and they recruited pirates and mercenaries [Hylo Visz §Breaking the blockade](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hylo_Visz); [Blockade of the Hydian Way §Smugglers take the lead](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blockade_of_the_Hydian_Way).

Their make-up is a [conflict] covered in the Coruscant file (Decision 4 there) [Hylo Visz §Breaking the blockade](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hylo_Visz); [Blockade of the Hydian Way §Smugglers take the lead](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blockade_of_the_Hydian_Way).

### E. Bounty hunters, the Great Hunt and the Blacklist
- **Type:** occupation / institutions
- **Era:** as tagged
- **Key facts:**
  - **The Republic and hunters during the war:**
    - Senate factions pushed for a ban on bounty hunter guilds. The Ministry of Justice taxed every credit collected and kept official payouts low. By the war's end most hunters avoided Republic work [Bounty hunter/Legends §The Republic and Bounty Hunters](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Bounty_hunter/Legends).
