## 1. Summary

Before TCW, Legends Mandalore was a poor, forested, clan world with no real central government. Its Mandalorians were warriors and mercenaries, and the title of *Mand'alor* sat empty after Jango Fett's death. Death Watch had been broken when Jango killed Vizsla (c. 42 BBY). In the Clone Wars, Mandalorians fought on both sides. Seventy-five of the hundred *Cuy'val Dar* trained the Republic's clone commandos. Spar ("Mandalore the Resurrector") raised the 212 Mandalorian Protectors from local police and ex-Death Watch, took MandalMotors, and fought for the Confederacy until Sidious sacrificed them at Norval II. Fenn Shysa became *Mand'alor* as the Empire arrived. TCW (from 2009–10) added a different picture: a pacifist New Mandalorian government under Duchess Satine Kryze, ruling from domed Sundari in a white-sand desert and leading the Council of Neutral Systems. In it Death Watch is a live terrorist army under Pre Vizsla, and Maul takes the planet in 20 BBY. Current Legends Wookieepedia welds the two together in sequence: Satine rules from 22 to 20 BBY, Maul's coup follows, and Spar then "wins out over the New Mandalorians" and joins the CIS in 20 BBY. That fix breaks primary dates (the Protectors fight for the CIS at New Holstice and Zaadja in 22 BBY). It also contradicts Traviss's "there's nothing to overthrow" Mandalore and needs Jango to have been an unrecognised *Mand'alor*. The cleanest fit with Jared's preference is to keep the pre-TCW Mandalore as the planet's real power. Under that option the New Mandalorians become a Republic-recognised enclave government in Sundari that the clans don't obey, and Pre Vizsla's Death Watch, Satine's death and Maul's coup all happen inside that enclave.

## 2. The pre-TCW picture

### 2.1 Background: Mandalore and the Mandalorians as written up to mid-2008

- **People, not a state.** The Mandalorians (*Mando'ade*, "children of Mandalore") were a warlike, nomadic, clan-based people of many species, often working as mercenaries and bounty hunters. Many had never visited Mandalore. They built no straight roads there so invaders could be ambushed ([Mandalorian/Legends, rev. 31 Jul 2008](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian/Legends?oldid=2102662)).
- **Culture.** The *Resol'nare* (Six Actions) defined a Mandalorian: wear armour, speak *Mando'a*, defend yourself and your family, raise your children as Mandalorians, help the clan, and rally to the *Mand'alor* when called. Farmers, factory workers, engineers and doctors were all "warriors at heart". Adoption was common (*aliit ori'shya tal'din*, "family is more than bloodlines"), and adulthood came at 13 with the *verd'goten* ([Mandalorian/Legends, rev. 31 Jul 2008, §Culture](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian/Legends?oldid=2102662)). Most of this culture was created by Karen Traviss ([same, §Behind the scenes](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian/Legends?oldid=2102662)).
- **Leadership.** The *Mand'alor* holds the rank only as long as he commands the warriors' respect ([Mandalorian/Legends, rev. 31 Jul 2008](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian/Legends?oldid=2102662)). By Jaster Mereel's time (c. 60 BBY), the old custom of the Mandalore's mask had died out and "Mandalorians merely flocked to powerful and charismatic leaders" ([Mandalore (title)/Legends, rev. 29 Jul 2008, §Tradition](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore_(title)/Legends?oldid=2099389)). Clan chieftains act as an unofficial senate and meet with the *Mand'alor* to decide things for Mandalore (*Sacrifice*; *Order 66*: [Mandalore/Legends §Government and economy](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore/Legends#Government_and_economy)). In *Imperial Commando: 501st* (Traviss, 2009, written before TCW's Mandalore episodes aired), an outsider tells Kal Skirata that Mandalorians have "this loose arrangement of clans" and a head of state "who only shows up part-time and doesn't make the rules". Her point is that Death Watch has nothing to overthrow ([Mandalorian Civil War (True Mandalorians)](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_Civil_War_(True_Mandalorians)), opening quote).
- **The planet.** It is an Outer Rim world in the Mandalore sector, with rainforests, forests and deserts and a population of 4 million. Keldabe is the capital, with Enceri and Kyrimorut also named ([Mandalore/Legends, rev. 2 Jul 2008](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore/Legends?oldid=2064881)). Keldabe is a fort-town on a granite hill in a bend of the Kelita River, among northern forests. It has the MandalMotors tower and the *Oyu'baat*, the oldest cantina, where clan leaders gather ([Keldabe/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Keldabe/Legends), citing *Order 66*, *A Practical Man*, *Imperial Commando: 501st*). The planet "eventually became part of the Republic (possibly c. 200 BBY), but was marginalized and grew destitute" ([Mandalore/Legends, rev. 2 Jul 2008](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore/Legends?oldid=2064881)).
- **Pre-war decline.** After Galidraan, "the Mandalorians were effectively ended as a fighting force". Individuals survived on Mandalore and in isolated pockets but had little influence until the Clone Wars ([Mandalorian/Legends, rev. 31 Jul 2008](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian/Legends?oldid=2102662)). With the civil war over, "the Mandalorians were all but extinct" ([Jango Fett/Legends, rev. 31 Jul 2008](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jango_Fett/Legends?oldid=2102027)), and Jango "found himself a subject-less Mandalore" ([Mandalore (title)/Legends, rev. 29 Jul 2008](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore_(title)/Legends?oldid=2099389)).
- **Scale of the civil war.** In *Imperial Commando: 501st*, the war swallowed Mandalore's full-time army and several prominent clans, but the two factions were small and Mandalorians living off-world, even in the Mandalore sector, were scarcely touched ([Mandalorian/Legends §The Mandalorian Civil War](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian/Legends#The_Mandalorian_Civil_War)). The May 2008 article notes, as an editor's observation, that every known battle of the war took place outside the Mandalore sector and "the scope of the Mandalorian government" ([Mandalorian Civil War, rev. 8 May 2008](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_Civil_War_(True_Mandalorians)?oldid=1965506)) [editorial observation, not a source statement].

**Conflict inside the primary record: Spar.** In *Order 66* (Traviss, 2008), Spar refuses Shysa's request to be Mandalore, never becomes Mandalore and never joins the Separatists; the novel ends with Shysa as "the first Mandalore since Jango Fett". Wookieepedia calls this "an anomaly": *The Complete Star Wars Encyclopedia*, *The Essential Atlas* and *Galaxy at War* all confirm Spar as Mandalore, and *Galaxy at War* treats the Shysa angle as rumour ([Spar §Behind the scenes](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Spar#Behind_the_scenes)) [conflict]. The majority version (Spar as *Mand'alor*) fits most sources.

**Where the pre-TCW sources stand.** *The History of the Mandalorians* (Abel Peña, *Star Wars Insider* 80, 2005) is the core source for the Protectors. It created Spar's role to explain Marvel's *Star Wars* 68 (1982), in which Shysa said he had served under Boba Fett ([Spar §Behind the scenes](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Spar#Behind_the_scenes); [Fenn Shysa, rev. 26 Jun 2008, §Behind the scenes](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Fenn_Shysa?oldid=2051689)).

## 3. The TCW picture

### 3.1 Background as current Legends Wookieepedia gives it [S-canon unless marked]

- **Where it came from.** The TCW season 2 episodes *The Mandalore Plot*, *Voyage of Temptation* and *Duchess of Mandalore* brought in "a significantly different portrayal of Mandalore": a barren white-sand desert, uninhabitable outside sealed cube cities of "staunch pacifists", with the domed capital Sundari ([Mandalore/Legends §Behind the scenes](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore/Legends#Behind_the_scenes)). [BTS] The "Creating Mandalore" featurette (TCW season 2 set) says the show's version stood for George Lucas's vision: Mandalorians as supercommandos rather than a multi-species nomadic horde, whose warlike ways had ruined their planet. The show also stated that Jango was a common bounty hunter who had come by Mandalorian armour, implying he and Boba were never Mandalorians ([Mandalorian/Legends §Behind the scenes](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian/Legends#Behind_the_scenes)). This contradicts the in-universe Legends record (Open Seasons) and is [BTS] only.
- **Legends' fix for the planet.** *The Essential Atlas* (2009) and *New Battlefronts: The Visual Guide* say the desert is only one of Mandalore's regions and the older forests still stand ([Mandalore/Legends §Behind the scenes](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore/Legends#Behind_the_scenes)). Sundari "stood alongside Mandalore's traditional capital of Keldabe" ([Sundari/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sundari/Legends)).
- **New Mandalorian origins.** In 738 BBY the Jedi led the Republic's Judicial Forces in the Mandalorian Excision. It bombed Mandalore, Concord Dawn, Ordo and Fenel into deserts, disarmed and occupied Mandalorian space, and set up a caretaker government from the failed peace movement (*The Essential Guide to Warfare*, 2012). From it came the pacifist New Mandalorians, "the new rulers of Mandalore" ([Mandalorian Excision](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_Excision)). They built bio-cube cities apart from the warrior clans and dropped armour and the *Resol'nare*; hardliners called them "the Faithless" ([New Mandalorians/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/New_Mandalorians/Legends)). *The Essential Atlas* has them holding ascendancy over the squabbling clans "for centuries". The New Mandalorian government also gave Mandalore Senate representation, "for the first time in the planet's history" ([Mandalore/Legends §War and reformations](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore/Legends#War_and_reformations)).
- **Two kinds of rule.** The New Mandalorians ran a centralised Ruling Council of ministers under a Prime Minister, subordinate to their own *Mand'alor*, who was chosen "in opposition to those who ruled the warrior clans" (*The Bounty Hunter Code*, 2013). Traditional Mandalorians (the "Old Mandalorians" or *Aka'liit*) kept their ways in places like Keldabe ([Mandalore/Legends §Government and economy](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore/Legends#Government_and_economy); [§War and reformations](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore/Legends#War_and_reformations)). Death Watch called the New Mandalorian leader the "Anti-Mandalore" ([Anti-Mandalore](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Anti-Mandalore)).
- **Satine and Jango.** Satine was named *Mand'alor* of the New Mandalorians at the end of the Great Clan Wars (*The Bounty Hunter Code*), "in opposition to Jango Fett" (*Order 66*), whom the New Mandalorians "refused to recognize" (*The Essential Guide to Warfare*) ([Mandalore/Legends §Peace under fire](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalore/Legends#Peace_under_fire)).
- **Death Watch's TCW form.** Pre Vizsla of Clan Vizsla, governor of the moon Concordia, publicly supported Satine while secretly leading Death Watch from the abandoned *beskar* mines. He allied with Dooku to take Mandalore ([Pre Vizsla/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Pre_Vizsla/Legends); [Concordia/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Concordia/Legends)). Under him Death Watch "carried on in secret for decades" and resurfaced after Tor Vizsla's death ([Tor Vizsla §Legacy](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Tor_Vizsla#Legacy)). [BTS] Dave Filoni borrowed the Vizsla name and the Death Watch symbol from the Expanded Universe for TCW season 2. The *Clone Wars Character Encyclopedia* then made the Death Watch insignia the symbol of Clan Vizsla, and Tor Vizsla's first name was only given in *The Essential Atlas* (18 Aug 2009) ([Tor Vizsla §Behind the scenes](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Tor_Vizsla#Behind_the_scenes)).
- **The famous clash with Traviss.** [BTS] Traviss announced on 8 Aug 2009 that *Imperial Commando 2* would be her last *Star Wars* novel. She then withdrew from it over issues including the continuity changes TCW brought in (her blog, 2 Dec 2009) ([Karen Traviss §Biography](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Karen_Traviss#Biography)). Wookieepedia's Legends Clone Wars pages affected by TCW carry a banner: "A definitive Legends Clone Wars timeline was never established by Lucasfilm" ([Template:TCWRetcon](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Template:TCWRetcon)). It sits on, among others, [Tor Vizsla](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Tor_Vizsla) and [Battle of New Holstice/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_New_Holstice/Legends).

### 3.3 How current Legends Wookieepedia joins the two

The current Legends pages put the two stories in sequence. "Kryze's untimely death would spark the end of her faction's power over Mandalore. Later, in 20 BBY," Spar arrives. Shysa persuades him to act as Jango's heir (*Order 66*), and he "won out over the New Mandalorians" (*Essential Atlas*), becomes Mandalore the Resurrector, and takes Mandalore into the CIS (*History of the Mandalorians*) ([Satine Kryze/Legends §Return of the old ways](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Satine_Kryze/Legends#Return_of_the_old_ways)). In this version Spar "overturned the New Mandalorians' position of neutrality". The New Mandalorians "never reclaim[ed] their lost dominance", while the clans and the Supercommando Codex came back to prominence (*Essential Atlas*) ([Mandalorian/Legends §Resurrection](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian/Legends#Resurrection)). The New Mandalorian movement is listed as "dissolved 20 BBY, following a Death Watch takeover" ([New Mandalorians/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/New_Mandalorians/Legends)). Death Watch's own article says its wish to return Mandalore to its warrior roots "would come to fruition" when the Protectors aligned the formerly neutral system with the CIS ([Death Watch/Legends §Clone Wars](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Death_Watch/Legends#Clone_Wars)).

The same articles still date the Protectors' battles at New Holstice and Zaadja to 22 BBY ([Battle of New Holstice/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_New_Holstice/Legends); [Battle of Zaadja](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_Zaadja)). That is before Spar's 20 BBY takeover in the sequential version, so the seam shows [conflict].

## 5. Reconciliation options

Jared's rules (24 Nov 2026): TCW material, characters included, stays if it doesn't conflict with Legends, and he prefers the pre-TCW Mandalorians. Already ruled: Maul survives Naboo and returns with Savage Opress and Mother Talzin, and dies on Tatooine in 16 BBY (*Old Wounds*). That ruling overrides the current Darth Maul/Legends page, which says Kenobi killed him "at some point afterward" (*Resurrection*) ([Darth Maul/Legends §Future erased](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Darth_Maul/Legends#Future_erased)).

### Option A: Clan Mandalore with a New Mandalorian enclave (pre-TCW as the frame; TCW kept as a faction story) **(recommended)**

**The shape.** Mandalore runs as the pre-TCW sources describe: a poor forest world of clans centred on Keldabe, with no government that can command Mandalorians. The New Mandalorians are a real but minority pacifist movement. They govern Sundari and its domed desert cities and the Kalevala connection, and the Republic and the Council of Neutral Systems recognise Satine as Mandalore's voice. The clans don't, and call her the "Anti-Mandalore" [inferred, built on [Anti-Mandalore](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Anti-Mandalore) and [Sundari/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sundari/Legends)]. Two "Mandalores" exist side by side. That is how current Legends already frames the title (row 8).

**Kept:**
- All of section 2.3: Jaster and Jango, Death Watch broken c. 42 BBY, the *Cuy'val Dar*, Kyrimorut, Spar as *Mand'alor* from early in the war, the 212 Protectors, MandalMotors in Keldabe, the BL droids, New Holstice and Zaadja in 22 BBY, Null/Kamino/New Bornalex in 19.42 BBY, Norval II, Shysa as *Mand'alor* in 19 BBY, the Imperial lease near Keldabe, Lorka Gedyc's post-war Death Watch.
- **Satine**: kept, as Duchess and leader of the New Mandalorians and the Council of Neutral Systems, Obi-Wan's old love. Her neutrality binds Sundari and the systems that follow her, not the clans. The Senate debate over the Mandalore Defense Resolution is real; the "rumour" that Mandalore is raising an army for the CIS is partly true, because Spar's clans are doing it, and that suits Dooku [inferred].
- **Pre Vizsla**: kept, as governor of Concordia (Clan Vizsla's province) and head of a Death Watch remnant. It is small and secretive (*ba'slan shev'la*), reviled by the clans, and strong enough for terrorism in Sundari but not a planetary war. A dozen Death Watch veterans who won't follow him join Spar instead [inferred, built on *Imperial Commando: 501st* and *Galaxy at War* via [Death Watch/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Death_Watch/Legends) and [Mandalorian Protectors (Spar)](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian_Protectors_(Spar))].
- **Almec**: kept, as the New Mandalorians' Prime Minister, the black-market scandal and jail, then Maul's puppet Prime Minister of Sundari in 20 BBY.
- **Bo-Katan**: kept, as Pre's lieutenant and leader of the Nite Owls who refuse Maul. After 20 BBY her small band is one more armed faction [inferred]. The Canon regency is not used.
- **Maul's takeover**: kept as the Takeover of Sundari, 20 BBY. The Shadow Collective topples the New Mandalorian government; Maul kills Pre and Satine and rules through Almec. Sidious kills Savage and captures Maul; Death Watch frees him; Zanbar and Dathomir follow and Talzin dies; the Collective is defeated (all [S-canon]). Maul's puppet hold on Sundari lapses when the Collective falls [inferred]. He goes on to die on Tatooine in 16 BBY per Jared's ruling.

**Dropped or demoted:**
- New Mandalorians ruling all Mandalore "for centuries", and Sundari as *the* capital. Keldabe stays the capital; Sundari is the New Mandalorians' capital.
- The Mandalorian Excision as the origin of Mandalorian pacifism and the deserts (*Essential Guide to Warfare*, 2012, built to fit TCW): keep as a regional backstory for the desert and the New Mandalorians, or drop. Decision 3.
- The Great Clan Wars as a war for the whole planet: reduce to clan feuds that Satine ended among the clans who joined her (Wookieepedia already flags the article's sources as conflicting).
- Jango "not a Mandalorian" ([BTS] line from the show): dropped.
- "Spar only took over in 20 BBY after Satine's death": dropped. Spar's rule of the clans runs alongside Satine's rule of Sundari from 22 BBY.

**Why it fits.** It keeps every primary date, including the 22 BBY Protector battles. It keeps every TCW character and the Maul arc. It matches *Imperial Commando: 501st*'s point that Death Watch can't topple a clan society: it can topple only Sundari's government, which is exactly what it does in TCW [inferred].

### Option B: Wookieepedia's sequence (TCW Mandalore to 20 BBY, then Spar)

**The shape.** Follow current Legends Wookieepedia: Satine's New Mandalorians govern the planet, neutral, from 22 to 20 BBY. Maul's coup follows in 20 BBY. Then Spar arrives, beats the New Mandalorians, and takes Mandalore into the CIS for the last months of the war ([Satine Kryze/Legends §Return of the old ways](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Satine_Kryze/Legends#Return_of_the_old_ways); [Mandalorian/Legends §Resurrection](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Mandalorian/Legends#Resurrection)).

**Kept:** all TCW events at full scale: Satine as ruler of Mandalore, Pre Vizsla's Death Watch as a real army, Almec, Bo-Katan, Maul holding the planet through Almec. All the Protector battles of 19.42 BBY and Norval II. The *Cuy'val Dar*.

**Dropped or changed:** the 22 BBY Protector presence at New Holstice and Zaadja (redate to 20 BBY or cut the Protectors from them). The early-war "Mandalore allied with the Confederacy" (Insider 80 via the 2008 Shysa page). *Imperial Commando: 501st*'s no-government Mandalore. Jango recognised as *Mand'alor* by Mandalore at large. Spar's Kamino escape before the war becomes a two-year gap before he acts. Maul's puppet rule has to end between his capture and Spar's arrival [unclear: no Legends source says how].

**Weakness:** it puts the TCW version first for 22–20 BBY, which goes against Jared's preference, and it still leaves the New Holstice and Zaadja dating broken.

### Option C: Pure pre-TCW Mandalore (TCW Mandalore dropped)

**The shape.** Only section 2.3. No New Mandalorians, Sundari, Satine, Concordia, Pre Vizsla, Bo-Katan or Almec.

**Kept:** the primary record intact. Death Watch is a scattered remnant and reappears only under Lorka Gedyc after the war.

**Dropped:** Satine (and Obi-Wan's Mandalorian romance), Pre Vizsla, Bo-Katan, Almec, the Council of Neutral Systems (or move it to another leader, which no source gives), the Defense Resolution and the Sundari story. Maul's 20 BBY arc loses its main target: Death Watch, the Darksaber duel, Satine's death and the Sidious duel on Mandalore would all need a new setting. The Shadow Collective could still form at Zanbar [inferred].

**Weakness:** it breaks Jared's "TCW stays if it doesn't conflict" rule for characters who can be fitted in, and it guts the Maul arc he has already kept.

### Recommendation

**Option A.** It is the only option that keeps both the pre-TCW Mandalore Jared prefers (a clan world, Spar and Shysa, the Protectors fighting for the CIS from 22 BBY) and the TCW characters he wants kept, and it keeps the Maul arc. The cost is mostly a matter of scale. TCW's Mandalore becomes one faction's enclave, its "centuries of New Mandalorian rule" and planet-wide Great Clan Wars are cut back, and the Excision-as-origin is optional. Every primary date stands.

## 6. Game notes, 22–19 BBY, under Option A (suggestions only)

These are design suggestions, not lore. The lore they rest on is cited above.

**Places**
- **Keldabe**: the capital in practice. Market days twice a week; the *Oyu'baat* cantina, where chieftains meet; the MandalMotors tower, which the Protectors and the "Mandalorian government" seize for the CIS during the war. A good hub for hiring Mandalorian mercenaries for either side ([Keldabe/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Keldabe/Legends)). From 19 BBY on, stormtroopers on leave from the City of Bone garrison.
- **Kyrimorut** (from 21 BBY): Clan Skirata's stronghold in the north. Hard to find, hostile to strangers, and a refuge for clone deserters ([Kyrimorut/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Kyrimorut/Legends)).
- **Sundari** and the desert domes [S-canon]: clean, ordered and policed by the Mandalorian Guard. The pacifist New Mandalorians, the Royal Palace and Peace Park. Black-market smuggling through the docks (c. 22 BBY); after 20 BBY, a Shadow Collective-run city with Almec as puppet ([Sundari/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sundari/Legends)).
- **Concordia** [S-canon]: the forested moon with abandoned *beskar* mines and Clan Vizsla's governor; a Death Watch hideout until Pre is exposed ([Concordia/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Concordia/Legends)).
- **Off-world**: New Holstice and Zaadja (21 BBY), Null, Kamino and New Bornalex (19.42 BBY), Norval II (late war). Places a player in either army might meet the Protectors.

**People a player might meet** (dates and fates from the tables above)
- *Clans and the CIS side*: Spar (Mandalore the Resurrector), Fenn Shysa and Tobbi Dala (constables turned Protectors), the other Protectors, and BL-series droids.
- *Republic side*: *Cuy'val Dar* on Kamino or with commando squads: Kal Skirata, Walon Vau, Mij Gilamar, Rav Bralor. Commandos who sing *Vode An* and swear in *Mando'a*. Some clones resent their Mandalorian roots once Mandalore sides with the CIS.
- *New Mandalorians* [S-canon]: Satine (to 20 BBY), Almec (Prime Minister, then jailed, then puppet), Tal Merrik (traitor senator, dies c. 22 BBY), Korkie and the Royal Academy cadets.
- *Death Watch* [S-canon at this scale]: Pre Vizsla (to 20 BBY) and Bo-Katan and her Nite Owls. Death Watch veterans of Galidraan who still hate the Jedi. Undercover Death Watch believers among the *Cuy'val Dar* (Dred Priest, Isabet Reau).
- *Underworld* [S-canon]: Shadow Collective gangs in 20 BBY; later Black Sun's Tyno Fabris at the *Oyu'baat* (c. 18 BBY).

**Hooks**
- A Republic agent investigates "Mandalore is raising an army for the Separatists". In Sundari it's a Death Watch lie; in Keldabe it's true (Spar). A good branch point between the two Mandalores.
- Hire Mandalorian mercenaries in Keldabe, and find the same clan has kin on the other side.
- A Jedi character meets Mandalorians who remember Galidraan (Spar's stated reason for siding with the CIS).
- 20 BBY: Sundari falls to Death Watch and the Shadow Collective. The clans in Keldabe watch, saying it is "not our government" [inferred].
- Late war: the Norval II mission, Sidious betraying his own Mandalorian allies. 19 BBY: the Empire offers the clans amnesty and paid work hunting Jedi.

**Tone**
- Speech: *Mando'a* phrases, "*ner vod*", the *Resol'nare*. No Mandalorian is ruled; the *Mand'alor* only leads those who choose to follow. A player who earns a clan's respect gains a family, not an employer.
