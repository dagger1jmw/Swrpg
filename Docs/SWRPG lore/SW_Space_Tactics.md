# Space Combat Tactics (starfighter and naval)

Legends lore reference for SWRPG. Researched 23 Nov 2026 by Claude through the Wookieepedia API. Draft for review.

**How it was built**
- Read the Legends members of Category:Starfighter tactics (about 70) and Category:Naval tactics (about 35).
- Kept the tactics that describe a general, reusable method. Named one-off stunts and later-era technology tricks are under Not used.
- Most generic tactics come from *The Rebel Alliance Sourcebook* (WEG), which presents them as standard doctrine with no era tie. Facts are given as plain lore; dated items are dated.

## Summary
- **Dogfighting is about position.** The tail is the killing position. The defender's tools are the break, jinking, the scissors, the stall and reversal turns. The attacker's counters are the Reverse Throttle Hop and the Tallon Roll.
- **Pairs beat singles.** Several of the most effective tactics need a wingman: the trap, the Under Split, the Corellian Slip, Feint and Backstab.
- **Fighters against capital ships:** hull skimming and trench runs get under the big guns; massed torpedo volleys on one shield facing (the nova flare) can bring a big ship down, and the counter is rolling the ship to show fresh shields.
- **Fleet doctrine:** the exchange, capping the T, the brawl and the fake form a basic rock-paper-scissors set. Breaking through the centre (a slash), pincers, feigned retreats into ambush, hit-and-run raids and fighter screens build on it.
- **Old Republic example:** the Vanjervalis Chain, a Mandalorian Wars-era Republic blockade of Hammerhead cruisers slaved to an Inexpugnable command ship.

### Starfighter: defensive manoeuvres
| Tactic | Key facts | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| The Break | Classic way to shake a pursuer: a sharp turn into the direction of the incoming attack so the attacker overshoots. Risks: a snap shot as the attacker passes, turning into the attacker's wingman's sights, and breaking too early or too late | [The Break/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/The_Break/Legends) |
| Jinking | Random slips, turns, dives and waggles to deny an attacker a solid lock. The attacker keeps his position, but the longer he works for a shot, the less aware he is of everything else, leaving him open to other fighters | [Jinking/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jinking/Legends) |
| Scissors | Timed sharp, twisting turns to bring an attacker into the defender's sights, usually right after the attacker overshoots (after a head-on pass or a successful break). Hard to time; needs constant watch on the opponent | [Scissors (tactic)/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Scissors_(tactic)/Legends) |
| Corkscrew Avoid | Spiral to dodge fire while holding a heading; one variation let a pilot launch torpedoes at a capital ship while being nearly impossible for its gunners to hit | [Corkscrew Avoid](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Corkscrew_Avoid) |
| Koiogran turn | Throttle forward with one hand, twist the stick with the other: the fighter loses forward momentum and loop-rolls onto a new heading. Used to escape pursuit and to turn weapons on the pursuer. Dangerous near terrain (one pilot clipped a treeline doing it in an unfamiliar ship). Appears in SWTOR: Galactic Starfighter | [Koiogran turn](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Koiogran_turn) |
| Silerian Stall | An abrupt halt so pursuers overtake and become targets | [Silerian Stall](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Silerian_Stall) |
| Power pivot | Lateral thrusters spin the fighter 180 degrees quickly | [Power pivot](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Power_pivot) |
| Luftberry circle | Purely defensive flight circle; each craft covers the one ahead so any attacker comes under fire. For slower or weaker craft, often bombers (helped by dorsal gunners), or obsolete fighters avoiding a dogfight | [Luftberry circle](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Luftberry_circle) |
| Terrain-following flying | Flying perilously close to a surface (mountains, cityscapes, asteroids, trenches); line-of-sight sensors read the ground instead of the ship. Needs superior awareness and reflexes. Also used to lose pursuers by weaving where they can't follow | [Terrain-following flying](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Terrain-following_flying); [Trench Run Defense](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Trench_Run_Defense) |

### Starfighter: attacking manoeuvres
| Tactic | Key facts | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Snap shot | A hasty shot as a target crosses in front. Low hit chance, but it can spook a rookie into a mistake; some pilots were unusually good at these high-deflection shots | [Snap shot/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Snap_shot/Legends) |
| Reverse Throttle Hop | Counter to a break: as the defender turns, the attacker pulls up above and decelerates, then drops back onto his tail. Needs split-second timing: too early and the defender loops around and wins; too late and the attacker overshoots | [Reverse Throttle Hop/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Reverse_Throttle_Hop/Legends) |
| Tallon Roll | Counter when about to overshoot a breaking target: level out, pull up hard, roll away from the turn and slide in behind. Happens in a pilot's blind spot, so hard to counter; hard to fly without becoming disoriented and overshooting anyway | [Tallon Roll/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Tallon_Roll/Legends) |
| Segnor's Loop | Run away from the target, then double back for a second pass | [Segnor's Loop](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Segnor%27s_Loop) |

### Starfighter: pair and group tactics
| Tactic | Key facts | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Trap | "The oldest, simplest, and most effective trick": a pair attacked from behind both break sharply; an attacker who follows ends up between them. Counter: feint at the leader, then Tallon Roll or Reverse Throttle Hop when the wingman slots in | [Trap (tactic)](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Trap_(tactic)) |
| Under Split | Lead fighter crosses in front of his wingman as bait; when the attacker follows, the wingman dives under the leader and cuts up for a belly shot. Dangerous for the lead; works best against green pilots | [Under Split/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Under_Split/Legends) |
| Corellian Slip | A friendly being chased is cleared by another friendly flying straight at both and veering off at the last moment to kill the pursuer. Supposedly invented by Corellian smugglers | [Corellian Slip](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Corellian_Slip) |
| Feint and Backstab | One flight runs head-on at the enemy and banks hard just before range, drawing them into pursuit; a second flight hanging back out of sensor range pounces | [Feint and Backstab/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Feint_and_Backstab/Legends) |
| Slip-jaws | Two fighters cross paths so close that their pursuers collide. Only possible for droid pilots or Force-aided pilots | [Slip-jaws/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Slip-jaws/Legends) |
| Battle spread | A pair flying side by side 200–300 m apart in hostile territory | [Battle spread](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_spread) |
| Over-under formation | Two lines of fighters, one ahead; once the enemy engages the first, the second springs the trap | [Over-under formation](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Over-under_formation) |
| Combat aerospace patrol | Fighters cover ground forces by intercepting air and space threats over the battle area; used by nearly every military and police force throughout history | [Combat aerospace patrol](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Combat_aerospace_patrol) |
| Voidfight | Fighting in deep space with no starlight; visual spotting is near useless, so pilots rely on instruments | [Voidfight](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Voidfight) |

### Fighters against capital ships
| Tactic | Key facts | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Hull skimming | Strafing about 5 m above a capital ship's hull for point-blank accuracy on vital systems while staying under its defensive guns | [Hull skimming](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hull_skimming) |
| Trench Run Defense | Hull-hugging runs that use a big ship's trenches for cover to deliver heavy ordnance to sensitive points. Works because capital ship guns are built to fight other capital ships. Usually a three-ship triangle: two rear fighters shield the lead, who carries the payload. Needs the enemy fighters dealt with first. Navies answered with faster interceptors and anti-fighter escort ships | [Trench Run Defense](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Trench_Run_Defense) |
| Nova flare | Massed torpedo or missile volleys from fighters aimed at specific points of one shield facing. Counter: the captain rolls the ship to show undamaged shields while the weakened side recovers; the fighters can re-orient onto the damaged side. The bigger and more frequent the volleys, the worse the odds for any ship regardless of size | [Nova flare](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Nova_flare) |
| Hit-and-run raids | Jump in, fire a few torpedoes, jump out. Forces the enemy to launch fighters (which take time to recover) and spread defences thin. Hard on pilots and fighters if kept up long | [Hit-and-hype raiding](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Hit-and-hype_raiding) |
| Ramming | Using a ship's own mass as a weapon; usually a last resort that can destroy the rammer too. Effect depends on the size of both ships and where they hit. In the Great Sith War, Satal Keto used mind control to drive Krath chaos fighter pilots into suicide rams (Battle of Koros Major) | [Ramming/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Ramming/Legends) |

### Fighter screens
| Tactic | Key facts | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Starfighter screen | A capital ship deploys its fighters between itself and enemy fighters (roughly a square) to intercept fighters and bombers, freeing the big ship to fight other capital ships. The screen can switch from defence to attack in a coordinated way, though this was rarely exploited | [Starfighter screen](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Starfighter_screen) |
| Screen formation (sphere) | A few fighters weave a sphere around a lightly defended ship, swapping positions so no one fighter is easy to target | [Screen formation](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Screen_formation) |

### Naval (capital ship) tactics
| Tactic | Key facts | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Exchange | Simplest naval tactic: two battle lines pass in opposite directions trading broadsides. Better shields and weapons win | [Exchange (tactic)](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Exchange_(tactic)) |
| Capping the T | A battle line crosses in front of the enemy line; every ship fires on the lead enemy ship in turn, while only the lead enemy ship can reply effectively | [Capping the "T"](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Capping_the_%22T%22) |
| Brawl | Desperation tactic when outnumbered or losing: lines rush each other and the fight breaks into ship-on-ship melee, two or three on one, even collisions. Usually won by the luckier commander | [Brawl/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Brawl/Legends) |
| Fake | Rush in as if starting a brawl to lure the enemy out of formation, then heel over into a line for a running broadside. Works against commanders who break formation; a commander who holds formation can wheel in behind the last ship and bracket it | [Fake](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Fake) |
| Broadside battle | Two ships side by side firing main batteries until one or both can't fight | [Broadside battle](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Broadside_battle) |
| Slash (line through the centre) | Drive a battle line through the middle of the enemy formation, firing broadsides both ways; only the enemy ships beside the line can reply. Suits ships with strong, redundant shields. Risk: if the lead ship is disabled, the line breaks up to avoid collisions and is exposed | [Ackbar Slash/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Ackbar_Slash/Legends) |
| Slashing the deck | Attack ships move along a vector that keeps them out of most of an enemy ship's guns, then double back and fire again. Dangerous if the target has fighter or capital ship support. Used by both sides in the New Sith Wars | [Slashing the deck](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Slashing_the_deck) |
| Pincer | Trap a target between attacking warships | [Pincer maneuver/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Pincer_maneuver/Legends) |
| Hull-shielded fighter launch | Close fast, angle the hull against the enemy's attack vector to shield fighters as they launch, then send them round the flank under cover of the hull. Obvious to experienced opponents; devastating against those who can't handle an unstructured attack | [Marg Sabl/Legends](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Marg_Sabl/Legends) |
| Feigned retreat (bait) | Pretend to retreat in disarray, or chase a small unit into a trap, to draw the enemy into an ambush or onto a waiting attack group jumping in from hyperspace | [Bothan fade](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Bothan_fade); [Beambaiting](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Beambaiting) |
| Feigned ignorance | A fleet pretends not to see a hidden enemy to make it reveal itself | [Friendly Hutt](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Friendly_Hutt) |
| Targeted barrage | Concentrate turbolaser fire on an enemy ship's weak points; often the easiest way to kill carriers | [Targeted barrage](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Targeted_barrage) |
| Carom shot | Ram one ship so it ricochets into another | [Carom shot](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Carom_shot) |
| Patrol triangle | Sensor ship on point, a stronger ship trailing about 100 km behind, and a heavy cruiser in the anchor position as command ship; patrols with minimum detection | [Triangle-High Forward](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Triangle-High_Forward) |
| Vanjervalis Chain (Old Republic) | Developed by the Republic Navy during the Mandalorian Wars. Hammerhead cruisers slaved to an Inexpugnable command ship's sensor package (built after the disaster at Serroco) form a long blockade chain; the package predicts enemy moves and adjusts the line. A fugitive ship couldn't break it. Weakness: the slaved control link could be hacked to turn the ships' guns | [Vanjervalis Chain](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Vanjervalis_Chain) |

## Game notes (optional, suggestions only)
- *Suggestion:* defensive moves (break, jink, scissors, stall, Koiogran turn) and their attacker counters (Reverse Throttle Hop, Tallon Roll) give a natural move-and-counter set for dogfight turns.
- *Suggestion:* the trap, Under Split and Corellian Slip make wingmen matter mechanically.
- *Suggestion:* exchange, capping the T, brawl and fake give a lore-backed rock-paper-scissors core for fleet formations.
- *Suggestion:* jinking's lore drawback (the attacker loses awareness) can be a penalty against third-party attacks.
