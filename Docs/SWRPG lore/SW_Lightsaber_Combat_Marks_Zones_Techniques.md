# Lightsaber Combat: Marks of Contact, Target Zones, Stances and Techniques (Legends)

Lore reference for SWRPG. Legends only. Written in our own words from the Wookieepedia Legends article "Lightsaber combat" (https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_combat/Legends), fetched 2026-11-18. Many of these terms originate in Legends reference books and the Wizards of the Coast roleplaying game material; treat them as Legends terminology, not Disney canon.

The "Game notes" lines are design suggestions for the build, not lore.

---

## 1. Marks of Contact

All Jedi forms shared the same marks of contact. Each describes what a strike sets out to do, how it is done, and what it achieves.

| Mark | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sun djem** | Disarming the opponent, ideally without seriously harming them. | The traditional aim of Form I (Shii-Cho). The Shii-Cho "Disarming Slash" was built around it. Saberstaff users were considered especially vulnerable to it. |
| **Shiim** | A minor wound from the edge of the blade. | Can land anywhere on the body and isn't tied to a target zone. |
| **Cho mai** | Cutting off the opponent's weapon hand. | |
| **Cho sun** | Cutting off the opponent's weapon arm. | |
| **Cho mok** | Cutting off a limb (an arm or a leg). | |
| **Shiak** | Stabbing the opponent. | |
| **Sai cha** | Decapitation, usually a sideswipe to the side of the neck. | Corresponds to Zone 2 and 3 attacks. |
| **Sai tok** | Cutting the opponent in two, usually at the waist. | Vertical bisections (Zone 1) also occurred. |
| **Mou kei** | A circular attack that cuts through several major limbs, crossing multiple zones. | The article suggests Kenobi's wounding of Vader on Mustafar could be read as a triple cho mok. |

Game notes:
- **Sun djem** = the existing disarm maneuver. Shii-Cho could get a bonus to it as a form trait (M6).
- **Shiim** matches the minor wound band (D-161). Narration can call low-margin saber wounds "shiim".
- **Cho mai / cho sun / cho mok / mou kei** = aimed strikes at the hand, arm or a limb (D-171), feeding severing. These are the M6 marks-of-contact techniques (D-187).
- **Sai cha / sai tok / shiak** = aimed strikes at the head or torso. These are the lethal marks: a clean hit is mortal.
- A skilled user's mark lowers the aim penalty for that mark's own location only (D-187).

## 2. Body Target Zones

| Zone | Target | Typical attack | Standard parry |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Head | Vertical chop, aimed at bisecting the opponent | Horizontal block |
| **2** | Right arm and side | Horizontal sideswipe | Vertical parry |
| **3** | Left arm and side | As Zone 2, reversed | Vertical parry |
| **4** | Back | Any attack from behind; described as almost always fatal | — |
| **5** | Right leg | Low sideswipe | Drop parry at the waist |
| **6** | Left leg | Low sideswipe | Drop parry at the waist |

Game notes:
- The zones map onto the existing hit locations: Zone 1 = head, Zones 2/3 = arm or torso side, Zones 5/6 = legs.
- **Zone 4 (back):** a strike that lands after a won take-position (getting behind the opponent), or against a fleeing or surprised opponent, could use a lethal location rule. Suggest recording this for M6 rather than building it now.

## 3. Stances

| Stance | Description |
|---|---|
| **Jedi Ready** | The most common Jedi stance, close to the Ataru guard: the blade held in a vertical parry on the dominant side. |
| **Defensive Neutral** | Like Jedi Ready but with the feet evenly spaced. It shows the most blade surface, for deflecting blaster bolts. |
| **Offensive Neutral** | Feet evenly spaced, the blade pointed at the enemy, showing the least blade area. |
| **Center of Being** | The blade held horizontally with the hilt just below the chin. Used both in combat and in meditation. |

Game notes:
- These could be the in-world names for the stance setting (D-182): Offensive Neutral = aggressive, Jedi Ready = balanced, Defensive Neutral = defensive, with Defensive Neutral getting its blaster-deflection flavor. Optional; numbers unchanged.

## 4. Maneuvers and Techniques

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Jung** | A 180-degree turn. |
| **Jung ma** | A 360-degree spin, used to build momentum. |
| **Shun** | A one-handed 360-degree spin. |
| **Sai** | Leaping over an attack aimed at the legs. |
| **Flowing Water** | Following an opponent's withdrawing blade so their own retreat draws your blade into them. |
| **Falling Leaf** | Spinning to cut at an opponent behind you, then turning back to face forward. |
| **Dulon** | A fast slash with the blade angled about 30 degrees upward; also the name of a solo training exercise. |
| **Kai-kan** | Re-enacting an earlier duel, as training. |
| **Riposte** | Striking right after deflecting or blocking an attack. |
| **Su ma** | Rotational movement in Ataru: jung su ma (spins), ton su ma (somersaults), en su ma (cartwheels). |
| **Tràkata** | Switching the blade off and on mid-fight to slip past a defense or draw the opponent into overextending. |
| **Solah** | The word used to yield during velocity drills. |

Game notes:
- **Riposte** fits a "counter after a successful defense" bonus, a natural Makashi and Djem So trait (M6).
- **Sai** (leaping a leg attack) is a defensive reaction against Zone 5/6 strikes.
- **Tràkata** could be a high-risk tactic: a large opening on success, with a big penalty if the opponent reads it.
- **Kai-kan** and **dulon** are training activities: dulon fits a solo drill block, kai-kan a study or review activity (compare the recording-review buff).
- **Solah** is flavor for ending a sparring bout.
