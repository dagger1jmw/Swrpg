# Lightsaber Construction, Crystals and Lightsaber-Resistant Materials (all Legends eras)

Legends lore reference for SWRPG. Researched 21 November 2026. Sources: Wookieepedia (Legends), as cited per entry. Draft for review.

## Summary
This file covers how Legends lightsabers were built and powered, the named crystal types and their stated effects, blade colours and what they signified, hilt and blade variants, and the materials that could resist a lightsaber (cortosis, phrik, Mandalorian iron/*beskar*, Sith alchemical alloys, ultrachrome, songsteel, armorweave, Yuuzhan Vong biotech and others). It spans all Legends eras, from the Force-imbued swords of the Force Wars (c. 24,500 BBY) and the first portable lightsabers (c. 15,500 BBY) to the Imperial Knights (c. 130–138 ABY). The key facts are these:
- A lightsaber was a hand-built hilt in which a power cell's energy was focused through one or more crystals and projected as a looping, contained blade. The crystal set the blade's colour and properties.
- Natural crystals came first from the Adega system, then mostly from Ilum (and, in the KOTOR era, Dantooine). Sith from Revan's time onward, and especially the Sith Empire of the Old Republic era, used red, usually synthetic, crystals.
- Cortosis could short out a blade; phrik, *beskar*, songsteel, ultrachrome, Sith alchemical alloys and Force-imbued weapons could resist it without shorting it.

### Lightsaber (overview and mechanics)
- **Type:** weapon / technology
- **Era:** c. 15,500 BBY onward (portable blades); modern self-powered hilt from c. 5000 BBY
- **Key facts:**
  - A typical hilt was a metal cylinder about 24–30 cm long, though hilts varied greatly because each was tailored to its maker (ref: *Jedi vs. Sith*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Mechanics")
  - Energy from a high-output diatium power cell passed through focusing lenses and energizers that turned it into plasma; the plasma was projected through one or more lightsaber crystals, which gave the blade its properties and allowed blade length and power to be adjusted (ref: *Luke Skywalker and the Shadows of Mindor*; *Jedi vs. Sith*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Mechanics")
  - The ideal number of crystals was three, though only one was required (ref: *Jedi vs. Sith*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Mechanics"). See Uncertainty.
  - Field energizers and modulation circuits in the emitter matrix made the plasma a coherent beam. The blade typically extended about a metre, then a containment field arced it back to a negatively charged fissure around the emitter and a superconductor returned the energy to the cell, completing the circuit (ref: *Fate of the Jedi: Outcast*). The containment field is also why two blades could clash instead of passing through each other. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Mechanics")
  - An inert power insulator protected the wielder from discharges (ref: *Star Wars: The Complete Visual Dictionary*); an activation stud ignited the blade (ref: JATM); a blade power adjustment knob could vary the blade's power (ref: *Star Wars: The Visual Dictionary*); a discharge energy cell could raise power output (ref: KOTOR II). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Mechanics")
  - The blade was massless and radiated no heat beyond visible light until it touched something solid. It cut almost anything, more slowly through denser material, and cauterised wounds so that they rarely bled heavily. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Cutting power")
  - When cutting dense material the blade's electromagnetic field caused resistance, giving a feeling of solidity; rarely, a solid material could pass through the field and short out the arc. The blade repelled most force fields, blaster bolts and other lightsaber blades. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Cutting power")
  - Unless specially made, a lightsaber shorted out when submerged in water; in rain it steamed but did not short out (ref: *Star Wars: Clone Wars* (2003) chapters 5 and 19; JATM). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Lightsaber-resistant materials")
  - A blade lost power only when the energy loop was broken, for example when it cut something, so a lightsaber could run for years between recharges (ref: *The Star Wars Sourcebook*, 1987). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Diatium_power_cell/Legends) ("Description")
- **Related:** Lightsaber hilt and components; Lightsaber crystals; Lightsaber-resistant materials
- **Uncertainty:** [unclear] on crystal count: the Lightsaber crystal page says "at the heart of every lightsaber was a single crystal" [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_crystal/Legends) (intro), while the Lightsaber page gives three as ideal and one as the minimum. The two can be read together (one primary crystal plus optional focusing crystals, as the Lightsaber hilt page describes), so this is not tagged [conflict].

### Lightsaber hilt and components
- **Type:** technology (components)
- **Era:** general (all eras of the modern lightsaber)
- **Key facts:**
  - Components were hand-selected by the builder and tuned to work in harmony (ref: *I, Jedi*). In order from the emitter: blade emitter shroud, magnetic stabilising loop and ring tuning flange (to keep the blade from burning through the hilt), blade arc tip, external blade power and length adjustment knobs, blade energy channel among cycling field energizers, and the crystal energy chamber holding focusing crystals, a focusing crystal activator and the primary crystal on its mount inside the energy gate (ref: *The Complete Visual Dictionary*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_hilt/Legends) ("Components")
  - The primary crystal mount connected to the diatium power cell, surrounded by a power field conductor, power vortex rings and an inert power insulator; a handgrip connector and belt ring completed a typical hilt (ref: *I, Jedi*; *The Complete Visual Dictionary*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_hilt/Legends) ("Components")
  - Apart from crystals, components were utilitarian and usually commercially available (ref: *I, Jedi*). Hilts were typically built over months, ideally in deep meditation on each component (ref: *I, Jedi*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_hilt/Legends) ("Components"; "Assembly")
  - Components could be mass-produced, but aligning them and the crystals needed a Force-guided hand; "no attempt to mass-produce lightsabers ever succeeded" (ref: *The Essential Guide to Warfare*, 2012). See Uncertainty. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_hilt/Legends) ("Assembly")
  - **Diatium power cell:** the standard power source, the same type used in blasters and glow panels (ref: *Young Jedi Knights: Lightsabers*); any type would do if it produced enough energy (ref: *I, Jedi*); it could be removed to power other devices (ref: *Shatterpoint*; *Clone Wars Gambit: Siege*). Charging it for the first time required the builder to open to the Force to integrate it with the other components (ref: *I, Jedi*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Diatium_power_cell/Legends) ("Description")
  - **Focusing lens:** regulated the beam so the blade ended at a set point; different lenses gave different properties. Named lenses include the vibration lens, Adegan lens, Beam Gem lens, Ossus dueling lens and pontite lens (no inline footnotes on the list). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Focusing_lens/Legends)
  - **Emitter matrix** (also "high energy flux aperture"): kept a tight magnetic grip on the beam so it extended to a set length (ref: *Star Wars Trilogy Sourcebook, Special Edition*). An inverted emitter matrix made the saber explode shortly after ignition, a trick a Jedi initiate named Petro once used on pirates. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Emitter_matrix/Legends)
  - **Blade emitter:** held the energy modulation circuits (amount and intensity of plasma) and the magnetic stabilising ring (blade length); the exterior emitter plate was a superconducting metal (ref: *The Complete Visual Dictionary*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blade_emitter/Legends) ("Overview")
  - **Emitter modifications (KOTOR II):** deflection emitters (better against blaster fire), disrupting emitters (unsteady beam that could cause temporary paralysis), phobium emitters (a heavier, more effective disrupting type), fencing emitters (better at parrying blades) (ref: KOTOR II; these are game items). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blade_emitter/Legends) ("Mechanics")
  - **Emitter guards** were in use as early as 4000 BBY; Exar Kun's hilt had a quadruple guard, and ornamental interlinked ring guards were popular among Jedi just after the Great Sith War (ref: *The Complete Visual Dictionary*; *Tales of the Jedi – Dark Lords of the Sith*; *Tales of the Jedi – Redemption*). Darth Baras's hilt combined a shroud with a triple guard (SWTOR, no inline footnote). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Blade_emitter/Legends) ("Exterior fittings")
  - **Pommel cap:** a heavy single-piece end cap that completed circuits and sometimes held a backup power cell (Dooku's and Obi-Wan Kenobi's first lightsaber); usually carried a belt ring, though Dooku's used a magnetic plate (1st app.: *I, Jedi*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Pommel_cap/Legends)
  - **Activation stud:** usually a prominent button, though Asajj Ventress concealed hers (ref: *The New Essential Guide to Weapons and Technology*). A pressure grip deactivated the blade when released; a locking activator kept it lit so the saber could be thrown (ref: JATM). A trapped grip shocked unauthorised users; a rare Force-activated saber had no button at all (ref: JATM). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Activation_stud)
  - **Bifurcating cyclical-ignition pulse ("waterproof casing"):** two crystals let a saber work underwater (ref: JATM; *Jedi vs. Sith*). Kit Fisto recorded how to add it in the Great Holocron, and the modification became widely used in the Clone Wars (ref: *Jedi vs. Sith*). By 24 ABY Praxeum students built it in as standard (no inline footnote). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Bifurcating_cyclical-ignition_pulse)
  - **Electrum detail:** a purely cosmetic gold electrum casing allowed only to high-ranking Jedi, an honour given to Masters (ref: JATM; *The New Essential Guide to Weapons and Technology*). Mace Windu and Darth Sidious had electrum hilts (ref: JATM). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Hilt variations"); [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Electrum/Legends)
  - A Cold War-era Jedi reading list for Padawans included a text titled "Crystal and Edge: Lightsaber Construction and Philosophy" (ref: *Star Wars: The Old Republic Encyclopedia*, 2012). Its contents are not described. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Crystal_and_Edge:_Lightsaber_Construction_and_Philosophy)
- **Related:** Lightsaber (overview); Construction practice; Lightsaber crystals
- **Uncertainty:** [conflict] on mass production. These are the versions:
  - **Version A, never succeeded:** "No attempt to mass-produce lightsabers ever succeeded" (ref: *The Essential Guide to Warfare*, 2012). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_hilt/Legends) ("Assembly")
  - **Version B, it was done:** Imperial engineers mass-produced components (still hand-assembled) on Kamino for the Imperial saber guard and the Starkiller clones (ref: TFU II); Desann manufactured lightsabers for the Reborn, described as an innovation (ref: *Jedi vs. Sith*); Sith apprentices under Kaan received stock lightsabers (ref: *Darth Bane: Path of Destruction*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Jedi Purge and reformation"; "Dark side resurgent"; "Aftermath and Sith rebirth")
  - **Fit:** Version B is attested by more, and more specific, sources across several eras. Version A may mean fully automated production without hand alignment, which Version B's "still had to be assembled by hand" does not contradict [inferred]. The developer decides.

### Construction practice and crystal sources (Jedi, Sith, others)
- **Type:** practice / ritual
- **Era:** general; dated examples from c. 22,800 BBY to 137 ABY
- **Key facts:**
  - Before installing a crystal, the builder imbued it with the Force by meditating on it for many days (ref: *Shadows of the Empire* novel). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("Preparation")
  - In the pre-Ruusan Order, crystals were gathered at Force-strong sites across the galaxy; after the New Sith Wars the Jedi limited harvesting to Ilum, whose crystals gave only blue and green blades; the New Jedi Order again used many kinds of gem (ref: *Jedi vs. Sith*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_hilt/Legends) ("Components")
  - **Ilum:** Jedi scouts found Ilum c. 22,800 BBY; its kathracite, mephite and pontite crystals bolstered the stock previously drawn from Adega (ref: *Jedi vs. Sith*). The Council built a temple and training centre there and kept the hyperlane secret (ref: SWTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi_Temple_%28Ilum%29/Legends) ("Construction")
  - **The Gathering:** a rite of passage in which a group of Initiates was taken, often to Ilum, to claim their first crystals; opening the sealed temple entrance with their combined focus on the Force was the first test (ref: *The Clone Wars* "The Gathering"). A Gathering for six younglings took place c. 20 BBY. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Gathering/Legends) (intro; "Process")
  - Most Padawans built their first lightsaber at Ilum in the period before the Great Jedi Purge (ref: JATM). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Ilum_crystal/Legends)
  - **Dantooine Crystal Cave** (KOTOR era): dug by kinrath near the Jedi Enclave; held Adegan crystals in blue, green and yellow; red crystals sometimes formed in unhatched kinrath eggs; rubat and bondar also occurred (ref: KOTOR; KOTOR II). An Ultima-Pearl was found there in 3951 BBY (ref: KOTOR II; date from *The New Essential Chronology*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Crystal_Cave_%28Dantooine%29) ("Description")
  - **Sith practice:** the Sith usually used red synthetic crystals (ref: JATM). Sith-made synthetic crystals gave a slightly more powerful blade when energised by the dark side, and Sith blades could occasionally "break" a Jedi blade (ref: *Jedi vs. Sith*). Darth Maul made his four crystals in a special furnace over several days (ref: *Darth Maul: Shadow Hunter*); Sidious instead supplied crystals to Tyranus and Vader (ref: *Attack of the Clones: The Visual Dictionary*; *Dark Lord: The Rise of Darth Vader*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("Preparation")
  - Under Kaan's Brotherhood of Darkness, apprentices received stock lightsabers; after the Rule of Two, apprentices built their own, often with supplied synthetic crystals (ref: *Darth Bane: Path of Destruction*; *Dark Lord: The Rise of Darth Vader*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Aftermath and Sith rebirth")
  - **Cleansing a dark crystal:** in 41 ABY Jaden Korr used meditation to banish the dark side from a dead Dark Jedi's red crystal and re-imbue it with the light, turning the blade yellow (ref: *Riptide*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("Preparation")
  - **Clone Wars:** Jedi lightsabers shifted from meditative exercise to heavy-duty weapons built for war (ref: *Labyrinth of Evil*). One lightsaber was sold at a Pons Ora storefront for 500,000 credits (ref: *The Clone Wars* "Missing in Action"). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Aftermath and Sith rebirth")
  - **Great Jedi Purge onward:** lightsabers were outlawed; Palpatine had many crystal sites razed or quarantined and sanctioned crystals on the market, such as Corusca gems (ref: *Jedi vs. Sith*; *Coruscant Nights II: Street of Shadows*). The early New Jedi Order relied on synthetic crystals (ref: *Jedi vs. Sith*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Jedi Purge and reformation")
  - By 14 ABY, Praxeum students could choose from pre-made hilt models, including Adept, Adjudicator, Arbiter, Avenger, Champion, Consul, Defender, Firebrand, Guardian, Praetor, Retaliator, Sentinel, Vanquisher and Vindicator (ref: *Jedi Knight: Jedi Academy*, a video game). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Hilt variations")
  - **Imperial Knights (c. 130–138 ABY):** each Knight built a standard-model hilt with a synthetic crystal using techniques from Palpatine's *Book of Anger*, in a process "bereft of ceremony"; blades were uniformly silver-white except customised ones, worked underwater, and were paired with cortosis gauntlets (ref: *Legacy Era Campaign Guide*; *Legacy* (2006) 0, 32). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Imperial_Knight_lightsaber) (intro; "Description"); [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Synthetic_lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("History")
  - **One Sith (c. 137 ABY):** many of their hilts were apparently made of a material similar to Yuuzhan Vong yorik coral, with synthetic crystals by tradition (no inline footnote). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Dark side resurgent")
  - Game mechanics (behind the scenes): in SWG, a crystal had to be "tuned" and could then be used only by that player (behind-the-scenes, game mechanic); SWTOR lets players gain crystals (behind-the-scenes). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("Behind the scenes")
- **Related:** Lightsaber crystals; Synthetic crystals; Timeline
- **Uncertainty:** [conflict] on where the Jedi got crystals before and around the KOTOR era. These are the versions:
  - **Version A, Adega first, then Ilum from c. 22,800 BBY:** before Ilum, most crystals came from Adega (ref: JATM) [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Adegan_crystal/Legends) ("History"); Ilum was found c. 22,800 BBY (ref: *Jedi vs. Sith*) [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi_Temple_%28Ilum%29/Legends) ("Construction").
  - **Version B, Dantooine crystals were popular "prior to discovery of the better source" on Ilum:** stated on the Dantari crystal page with no footnote (1st app.: KOTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Dantari_crystal/Legends)
  - **Fit:** Version A fits better: it is footnoted, and the Ilum temple page dates the temple to the Order's formative years (citing *Jedi vs. Sith* and SWTOR). Version B is unfootnoted. The developer decides. (A third, gray-area version, that the Jedi used rubat exclusively before Ilum, contradicts Version A and is under "Not used".)

### Lightsaber crystals: general properties and families
- **Type:** material (category)
- **Era:** general
- **Key facts:**
  - Lightsaber crystals (also "jewels", "focusing crystals" or "kyber crystals") were crystals, gems, stones or other objects used as a lightsaber's energy source. Not all were true crystals: some users substituted remains, glass shards or micro-machinery (no inline footnote). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_crystal/Legends) (intro)
  - Often, the rarer the crystal, the more powerful its effect on a lightsaber (ref: KOTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("Types of lightsaber crystals")
  - Crystals could be **compressed**, giving a pulsating blade, or **unstable**, giving a swirling, sparkling core (ref: TFU). Crystals of different colours could be combined; Galen Marek combined a green and a deep-blue crystal for an aqua blade (ref: TFU). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("List of crystal colors")
  - **Compressed energy crystals (CECs):** included Luxum, Ilum and Adegan crystals and Corusca gems (ref: *Coruscant Nights II*). Some gave a pulsating blade with a thinner core that seemed more precise. CECs were also the core of Ves Volette's light sculptures. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Compressed_energy_crystal)
  - **Adegan family:** found in and around the Adega system [gray-backed, see below]; the most common crystal of the Old Republic, used for thousands of years (ref: JATM). First found long ago on Ossus by an ancient Jedi stronghold (ref: *Fact File* 119). The name was applied to all such crystals, including those from Halm, Mygeeto, the Cularin belt and later Ilum (ref: various; SWTOR for Ilum). When induced by resonant frequencies they emitted powerful bursts of light that the rest of the saber shaped into a beam (ref: *Dark Empire* endnotes). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Adegan_crystal/Legends) (intro; "History"; "Properties")
  - Order of rarity within the Adegan family, commonest first: kathracite, relacite, danite, mephite, pontite (no inline footnote for the full order; danite's position is footnoted to *Tales of the Jedi Companion*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Adegan_crystal/Legends) ("Types of Adegan crystal"); [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Danite/Legends)
  - Mephite and pontite gave the best blades and were similar in structure to Ilum crystals, but Ilum crystals were far more common (ref: JATM). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Ilum_crystal/Legends)
  - [gray] The crystals were found "in and around" the Adega system, and a weak Force signature from Adegan crystals in a lightsaber could be sensed by any Jedi. *Star Wars: Force and Destiny Core Rulebook* (published 30 July 2015), cited at [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Adegan_crystal/Legends) (intro; "Properties"). The location is backed by the *Dark Empire* endnotes (1990s), cited on the same page (infobox). The Force signature: fits, unconfirmed.
  - [gray] During the Empire's rise, Palpatine obstructed access to all lightsaber-capable crystals by closing or destroying known sources and removing crystals from circulation. *Force and Destiny Core Rulebook* (30 July 2015), cited at [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Adegan_crystal/Legends) ("History"). Backed by *Jedi vs. Sith* (2007) and *Coruscant Nights II* (2009): [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Jedi Purge and reformation").
- **Related:** Crystal table; Synthetic crystals; Colours
- **Uncertainty:** none beyond the tags above.

### Synthetic lightsaber crystals
- **Type:** material
- **Era:** first use between 6900 BBY and 3961 BBY (as the page's infobox puts it); standard among Sith from Revan onward
- **Key facts:**
  - A synth-crystal was made artificially instead of forming geologically. They were usually red because of the forging process, but could be made in any colour through manipulation of the process or the Force; Luke Skywalker's second lightsaber had a green synth-crystal (ref: JATM). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Synthetic_lightsaber_crystal/Legends) (intro)
  - The Sith learned to energise, magnetise and modify synth-crystals with the dark side in special furnaces; the result gave more powerful blades and was easier to augment, and Jedi were strongly discouraged from using them (ref: JATM). Red blades from them were nicknamed "bloodshine blades" (no inline footnote). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Synthetic_lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("Description")
  - **Creation:** made in a geological compressor from high-carbon minerals, baked for 24 hours to four days while the maker meditated on it (ref: JATM; *Darth Maul: Shadow Hunter*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Synthetic_lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("Creation")
  - **Variants:** *standard* (more powerful than natural, rare chance to "break the blade" of a standard saber by overloading it, but less manoeuvrable and less stable) (ref: JATM; *Jedi vs. Sith*); *compressed* (a failed replication; thinner, more precise blade with regular energy oscillations) (ref: JATM; TFU); *unstable* (a failed replication; crackling, sparking blade that did more damage but was prone to shorting out) (ref: JATM). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Synthetic_lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("Variants")
  - **History:** red crystals were used in the Old Sith Empire (Karness Muur) (ref: *Knights of the Old Republic* 25), but by 5000 BBY most Sith wielded Sith swords (ref: *Tales of the Jedi – The Golden Age of the Sith* 2). Exar Kun's followers kept their Jedi sabers; the Dark Jedi Haazen was the first to take up a red blade before the Empire's resurgence (ref: *Knights of the Old Republic* 32). Revan and Malak made red crystals widespread, and red became the mark of darksiders (ref: KOTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Synthetic_lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("History")
  - Darksiders used synth-crystals mainly to stay hidden, since natural crystal sites were frequented by Jedi; the practice became tradition and was seen as greater mastery (ref: JATM). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Synthetic_lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("History")
  - From the Sith Emperor's reign over the Sith Empire, Sith used synthetic crystals, so their blades were red (ref: SWTOR; JATM). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("Significance of colors")
  - Not every red crystal was synthetic: red Adegan crystals existed (Darth Vader used one in a lightsaber) (ref: *Official Star Wars Fact File* (2013)), and red crystals formed in kinrath eggs on Dantooine (ref: KOTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Synthetic_lightsaber_crystal/Legends) ("History")
- **Related:** Construction practice; Colours; Imperial Knights
- **Uncertainty:** [unclear] on when synth-crystals were first made: the page's infobox gives only "between 6900 BBY and 3961 BBY" and says the start is unknown.

### Blade colours and their meaning
- **Type:** convention / property
- **Era:** general; dated examples 3956 BBY onward
- **Key facts:**

### Training lightsaber

- **Era:** general; Old Republic to New Jedi Order
- **Key facts:**

Initiates traditionally were not ready for a real saber until they could perform the *Sevinte* cadence with strikes within a hand's breadth of the opponent (ref: *The Jedi Path*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Training_lightsaber/Legends)
  - In the New Jedi Order they were given to grown Padawans and training droids (ref: *Jedi Knight: Jedi Academy*); the Lost Tribe of Sith also used them (ref: *Fate of the Jedi: Omen*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Training_lightsaber/Legends)
- **Related:** Bondar; Adegan crystals
- **Uncertainty:**
  - [conflict] on how a training saber limited damage. These are the versions:
    - **Version A, permanent low-power setting:** the weapon was so weak someone could grab the blade and suffer only burns and bruises (ref: *Yoda: Dark Rendezvous*, 2004). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Blade variations")
    - **Version B, not low power but an intensified containment field:** the page calls the low-power idea a common misconception; a trained Jedi could readjust the field for emergency use (no inline footnote for this paragraph). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Training_lightsaber/Legends)
    - **Fit:** Version A is footnoted to a novel; Version B is unfootnoted. Neither is clearly more authoritative. The developer decides.
  - [conflict] on which crystal training sabers used. These are the versions:
    - **Version A, bondar (stun):** in the Old Republic era (ref: KOTOR, 1st app.). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Bondar)
    - **Version B, low-grade Adegan crystals (kathracite, relacite, danite):** unfootnoted on [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Adegan_crystal/Legends) ("Types"); the [gray] *Force and Destiny Core Rulebook* (2015) says danite or kathracite ([source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Danite/Legends)).
    - **Fit:** the versions may describe different eras (Version A is tied to the Old Republic; Version B is general) [inferred]. Version A has the firmer era anchor. The developer decides.

### Before the lightsaber: Force-imbued swords, Forcesaber and the First Blade
- **Type:** weapons / history
- **Era:** Force Wars and First Great Schism (c. 24,500 BBY) to c. 15,500 BBY
- **Key facts:**
  - **Force-imbued sword ("Jedi katana"):** a solid metal blade with no power source that glowed with the Force (lightning-like striations, discolouration or fire-like effects) (ref: *The Essential Guide to Warfare*; *The Jedi Path*; *The Essential Atlas*). Used by Jedi before the protosaber, and by followers of the Bogan and the Legions of Lettow; in use in the Force Wars and the First Great Schism c. 24,500 BBY (ref: *Jedi vs. Sith*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Force-imbued_sword/Legends) (intro; "History"; "Description")
  - **Forging a Force-imbued sword:** the initiate hammered a high-carbon bar while channelling the Force into a reinforced lattice; set a crystal in the hilt; meditated until crystal, blade and smith "became as one"; then channelled energy until the blade glowed permanently. Blade or crystal could shatter from flaws or lack of skill (ref: *The Essential Guide to Warfare*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Force-imbued_sword/Legends) ("Creation")
  - [gray] The Force-imbued blade was first conceived on Tython by the Je'daii Order. *Force and Destiny Core Rulebook* (30 July 2015), cited at [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Force-imbued_sword/Legends) ("History"). Backed by *Star Wars: Dawn of the Jedi* 0 (2013): at Vur Tepe, the Je'daii temple of metallurgy on Tython, technology let early Force users imbue their katana with the Force ([source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Vur_Tepe) "History").
  - [gray] The Je'daii initially used Vur Tepe to imbue swords with the Force. *Nexus of Power* (published 17 March 2016; edge of window, strict rule applied), cited at [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Force-imbued_sword/Legends) ("Creation"). Backed by *Dawn of the Jedi* 0 (2013), as above ([source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Vur_Tepe) "History").
  - **Vur Tepe ("the Forge"):** a rust-coloured ziggurat on the lip of an active volcano on the Tythos Ridge whose forges ran on lava heat; in 25,793 BBY its overseer was Master Tem Madog (ref: *Dawn of the Jedi* 0). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Vur_Tepe) ("History")
  - **Forcesaber:** a Rakatan invention channelling dark-side energy through laboratory-grown black crystals into an ignitable energy blade; Force Hounds built them with special crystals and alchemy; some Rakatan versions resembled lightsaber pikes (1st app.: *Dawn of the Jedi: Force Storm* 1; ref: *Dawn of the Jedi* 0). The protosaber was based on it (ref: *Dawn of the Jedi* 0). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Forcesaber); [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Force-imbued_sword/Legends) ("History")
  - **First lightsabers:** after the Force Wars the Jedi combined offworld technology with a forging ritual to "freeze" a laser beam (ref: *The New Essential Chronology*). By the Duinuogwuin Contention, c. 15,500 BBY, "frozen blaster" research produced the first portable blades: unstable, power-hungry, belt-powered and overheating, so little more than ceremonial (ref: *The New Essential Chronology*; *Jedi vs. Sith*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Origins")
  - **First Blade:** the earliest known lightsaber, built on Tython by a Jedi Master known only as the Weapon Master and kept by droids for millennia (ref: SWTOR). During the Force Wars, Rajivari proved worthy of it and engraved its hilt with the location of his knowledge reservoir; in the Cold War the Scorekeeper's Herald recovered it and fitted it with a crystal from the Tythonian Forge (ref: SWTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/First_Blade); [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Origins")
  - Despite the first lightsabers, Jedi kept using Force-imbued swords as late as the First Great Schism (ref: *The Essential Guide to Warfare*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Origins")
- **Related:** Protosaber; Sith swords; Timeline
- **Uncertainty:** [conflict] on when the first lightsaber existed. These are the versions:
  - **Version A, after the Force Wars, first portable blades c. 15,500 BBY:** (ref: *The New Essential Chronology*; *Jedi vs. Sith*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Origins"); the Lightsaber hilt page also dates the first hilts to about 15,500 BBY ([source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber_hilt/Legends) intro).
  - **Version B, the First Blade already existed during the Force Wars:** Rajivari retrieved it then (ref: SWTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/First_Blade) ("History")
  - **Fit:** Version A fits more sources (two reference books plus the hilt page and the Force-imbued sword page, which has Jedi using swords through the Force Wars and First Great Schism). Version B is SWTOR content, which is fully Legends. The Lightsaber page itself lists both without reconciling them. The developer decides.

### Sith swords and Sith alchemy
- **Type:** weapon / technique
- **Era:** Sith swords 6900 BBY to c. 5000 BBY in the Sith Empire; later relics to 14 ABY
- **Key facts:**
  - Sith swords (warblades, daggers) were made by metallurgical and arcane means that turned base metals into an alchemical alloy with an affinity for the dark side; they blocked lightsabers and deflected blaster bolts much as a lightsaber did (ref: *The Dark Side Sourcebook*). Weight 3–6.5 kg and price 3,000–6,000 credits (game stats: *Threats of the Galaxy*; *The Dark Side Sourcebook*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sith_sword) (infobox; "Description")
  - A dark-side user could alter the blade's molecular structure and reverse its magnetic polarity, turning it into a high-temperature superconductor that deflected bolts; its alloy resisted lightsabers much like cortosis and phrik, and its polished surface reflected lasers; it also amplified the wielder's dark-side power (no inline footnote for this paragraph). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sith_sword) ("Description")
  - The first Sith sword may have predated the earliest lightsabers (ref: *The New Essential Guide to Weapons and Technology*); developed by the Sith species and adopted by the Exiles on Korriban (ref: *Book of Sith*). Naga Sadow and Ludo Kressh dueled with them (ref: *Tales of the Jedi – The Golden Age of the Sith* 2); Sith Elite Warbots carried them in the Great Hyperspace War (ref: *Threats of the Galaxy*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sith_sword) ("History")
  - Sith alchemy was used in the first Sith Empire to augment metals against the lightsaber, and the Sith also forged cortosis into Sith swords; its most notorious later use was in Darth Vader's armour (no inline footnote). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Lightsaber-resistant materials")
  - Force alchemy was also practised by some Je'daii on Tython, including alchemical metallurgy to alter traditional and Force-based weapons (ref: *Dawn of the Jedi: Into the Void*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sith_alchemy/Legends) ("Je'daii Order of Tython")
  - **Sith Tremor Sword:** a vibroblade like those of the ancient Sith Lords of Ziost (ref: *KOTOR Campaign Guide*); awarded to Bladeborn "masterblades" who had killed at least ten lightsaber wielders; laced with cortosis (ref: KOTOR II Prima guide). Revan's Empire issued them to Dark Jedi and Sith troopers (ref: KOTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sith_Tremor_Sword); [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Sith_sword) ("History")
- **Related:** Cortosis; Force weapon
- **Uncertainty:** none

### Cortosis
- **Type:** material
- **Era:** widespread weapons c. 3956 BBY; New Sith Wars mining on Apatros; Clone Wars; Imperial Knights c. 130–138 ABY
- **Key facts:**
  - Cortosis ore was very rare, brittle and fibrous; its conductive properties made lightsabers short out temporarily on contact, though repeated strikes could still cut it; it also resisted blaster fire (no inline footnote). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis/Legends) (intro)
  - Mining it was dangerous and expensive: pure unrefined ore was for unexplained reasons energised and lethal to bare skin; dust caused hair loss, sickness and death without filters; energy tools were useless on it, so hydraulic jacks were used and wore out quickly; pockets of combustible lethane gas formed near deposits (ref: *Darth Plagueis*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis/Legends) ("Overview")
  - Known deposits or mining: Apatros (Outer Rim Oreworks; Darth Bane's birthplace), Obredaan, Bal'demnic (Separatist mining in the Clone Wars), Duro, Katanos VII, Nirauan (no inline footnotes). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis/Legends) ("History")
  - **Cortosis-weave:** a swordsmithing technique for high-quality vibroblades expected to face lightsabers; weapons need little cortosis, armour much more, and enough cortosis in armour makes a saber deactivate on contact; the art of bonding cortosis to blades was first perfected by Echani weaponsmiths before the Mandalorian Wars (no inline footnote; 1st app.: *Jedi Knight II: Jedi Outcast*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis-weave)
  - In the Jedi Civil War era personal energy shields made blasters partly obsolete, so melee weapons became common, and almost all were made with cortosis woven into the blade (ref: KOTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Jedi Civil War")
  - **Cortosis swords** were widely available c. 3956 BBY; mixed cortosis made blades resistant, while pure cortosis blades could short a saber "for minutes at a time" (no inline footnote; 1st app.: *Edge of the Empire Core Rulebook*, 2013). Trade Federation assassin droids used them in 32 BBY. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis_sword/Legends)
  - **Cortosis shield:** refined of impurities, as good as cortosis-weave armour; a hit shut the saber off and it had to be restarted. Darth Desolous and his Pau'an army used them; the Yinchorri used them in their raid on the Jedi Temple (ref: *Jedi Council: Acts of War* 2). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis_shield/Legends)
  - **Cortosis staff:** a Morgukai weapon about a metre long, laced with cortosis, with a small plasma blade at the top and an electrical generator that powered it and released shocks; Tholme first met one on Ryloth in 30 BBY (1st app.: *Star Wars* (1998) 42). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis_staff/Legends)
  - **Cortosis gauntlet:** arm braces with woven cortosis; a blade touching one shorted out "for several minutes". Micah Giiett taught Padawans with a cortosis vambrace; Roan Fel's Imperial Knights all wore them. 1 kg, 1,500 credits (game stats: *Legacy Era Campaign Guide*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis_gauntlet/Legends)
  - **Cortosis shot:** a slugthrower round Cad Bane used in 21 BBY to short a Jedi's blade; he said it was expensive and hard on the barrel (no inline footnote). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis/Legends) ("Cortosis Weapons")
  - Other users: shadowtrooper armour, C-B3 cortosis battle droids, the Jensaarai, Imperial Palace walls, ship hulls, riot stormtrooper electrostaffs, Galak Fyyar's armour, Nyss Nenn's cortosis-coated vibroblades (no inline footnotes). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis/Legends) ("Overview"; "History")
  - The purified form, called "Cortosis-Shield", was used on Imperial Knight gauntlets and was said to be more durable than *beskar*; only lightsabers using a special crystal mined on Mestare were immune to cortosis (no inline footnote). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis/Legends) ("Overview")
- **Related:** Phrik; Mandalorian iron; Weapons and armor file (anti-Jedi gear)
- **Uncertainty:** [unclear] on how long a blade stays off after touching cortosis: "temporarily" / "a short period" ([source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis/Legends)), "minutes at a time" ([source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis_sword/Legends)), "several minutes" ([source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis_gauntlet/Legends)), or until restarted ([source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Cortosis_shield/Legends)). None of these statements is footnoted; they are vague rather than contradictory. The Mestare crystal is named only in this unfootnoted sentence; no page for it was checked.

### Phrik
- **Type:** material (metallic compound)
- **Era:** devised before the Mandalorian Wars; Clone Wars; Galactic Civil War
- **Key facts:**
  - One of the few substances that resisted lightsabers; often gold-sheened when refined; used for very light, durable armour (ref: *Lords of the Expanse*) and melee weapons. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Phrik/Legends) (intro)
  - Made by combining ore from the phrikite mines of Gromas 16 with tydirium (ref: Wizards.com "Kyle Katarn's Tale"); durable, light and very expensive, so rarely used in war. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Phrik/Legends) (intro)
  - The Arkanian Gorman Vandrayk devised phrik alloy before the Mandalorian Wars and may have invented it; the extinct Fenelar used it in armour millennia before the Galactic Civil War (ref: Wizards.com). It was used in IG-100 MagnaGuard electrostaffs for its lightsaber resistance and ability to disperse electricity, in Darth Sidious's lightsaber (ref: *Revenge of the Sith: The Visual Dictionary*) and in dark trooper armour (ref: *Dark Forces*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Phrik/Legends) ("History")
  - Malleable while smelting and combinable with other ores and alloys. The Empire nationalised phrik mining where it found it; small mines supplied others, notably Rendili (ref: *Lords of the Expanse*). A phrik container survived Alderaan's destruction (ref: *Battlefront: Renegade Squadron*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Phrik/Legends) ("History")
  - Unlike cortosis, phrik did not short out a blade. [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Lightsaber-resistant materials")
- **Related:** Guard shoto; Lightsaber pike; Electrostaff (weapons file)
- **Uncertainty:** none

### Mandalorian iron (*beskar*)
- **Type:** material
- **Era:** discovered after the Taung conquest of Mandalore c. 7000 BBY; through 41 ABY
- **Key facts:**
  - An extremely durable iron ore whose only known sources were Mandalore and its moon Concordia (ref: *Legacy of the Force: Sacrifice*; *The Clone Wars: New Battlefronts: The Visual Guide*). It could withstand lightsaber blows (ref: *Sacrifice*) and was prized above durasteel or cortosis (ref: *Star Wars Insider* 86). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Beskar/Legends) (intro; "Description")
  - Mandalorian smiths closely guarded forging techniques. Carbon added in the foundry formed a molecular cage making it lighter; without carbon it was much heavier; repeated folding strengthened it (ref: *Insider* 86; *Republic Commando: Order 66*). It could be made into plates, laminate, wire, mesh, micronised particles, foam or transparent film (ref: *Imperial Commando: 501st*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Beskar/Legends) ("Description"; "Usage")
  - *Beskar* armour withstood blunt force, lightsaber strikes and repeated blaster fire, though some impact still reached the wearer (ref: *Legacy of the Force: Revelation*). It was also used for *beskad* sabers, *kal* knives, crushgaunts, saber hilts that resisted other blades, and shields (ref: *Sacrifice*; *The Bounty Hunter Code*; *Book of Sith*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Beskar/Legends) ("Usage")
  - Arca Jeth sealed Freedon Nadd's tomb on Dxun with a *beskar* slab; Exar Kun got through only with repeated strikes at his saber's highest power (ref: *Tales of the Jedi – Dark Lords of the Sith* 2). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Beskar/Legends) ("Early history")
  - By the Cold War between the Republic and the Sith Empire (which followed the 3653 BBY Sacking of Coruscant, per [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) "Great Galactic War"), Mandalorians saw *beskar* as the ideal armour material against lightsaber-armed Force users (ref: SWTOR). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Beskar/Legends) ("Early history")
  - The Empire strip-mined Mandalore's shallow veins; a new deep lode was found near Enceri in 40 ABY (ref: *Legacy of the Force: Sacrifice*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Beskar/Legends) ("A Mandalorian resource"; "War and resurgence")
- **Related:** Darksaber; Weapons and armor file (Mandalorian armour)
- **Uncertainty:** none

### Other resistant materials
- **Type:** materials
- **Era:** various (see table)
- **Key facts:**
  - **Songsteel:** extraordinarily light, luminescent silver metal for masterwork swords and staves; very tedious to forge; highly resistant to lightsabers like phrik and cortosis; Darrus Jeht blocked two Masters' combined attacks with a songsteel sword; deposits were found in the Cularin asteroid belt (ref: Wizards.com Living Force campaign). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Songsteel/Legends)
  - **Ultrachrome:** silver, superconductive starship armour around the Great Sith War; reflected blaster and slugthrower fire; immune to metal-eating fungi; resisted lightsabers by spreading their energy across the whole mass, but enough energy melted the whole piece; Imperial Inquisitors wore it (ref: *The Jedi Path* for Inquisitors). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Ultrachrome/Legends)
  - **Armorweave:** armoured cloth that dissipated blaster bolts and had at least limited lightsaber resistance; worn by bounty hunters, the counts of Serenno (ref: *Revenge of the Sith* novelization), Grievous and his MagnaGuards (ref: *Labyrinth of Evil*), Vader's cape (ref: *Dark Lord*), Zam Wesell (ref: *Fact File* 53). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Armorweave/Legends)
  - **Force weapon:** a Force ability imbuing an unpowered weapon so it hit harder and could block lightsabers unharmed (ref: *Tales of the Jedi – Dark Lords of the Sith* 1); used by the Zeison Sha (ref: *Hero's Guide*), Vodo-Siosk Baas with his walking stick against Exar Kun (ref: *Tales of the Jedi*), Felucian natives (ref: TFU); Force-imbued weapons could damage dark side spirits (ref: *The Dark Side Sourcebook*). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Force_weapon)
  - [gray] The Je'daii of Tython used the Force weapon ability. *Force and Destiny Core Rulebook* (30 July 2015), cited at [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Force_weapon). Backed by *Dawn of the Jedi* 0 (2013) via [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Vur_Tepe) ("History").
  - **Yuuzhan Vong biotech:** vonduun crab armour resisted blasters, slugthrowers and lightsabers; modified sets were used by the New Jedi Order for dueling practice by 40 ABY (no inline footnotes). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Vonduun_Skerr_Kyrric) ("Technical details"; "Advantages"). Amphistaffs could clash with lightsabers repeatedly without damage (no inline footnote). [source](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber/Legends) ("Lightsaber-resistant materials")
- **Related:** Cortosis; Phrik; Sith swords
- **Uncertainty:** the Lightsaber page's resistant-materials list is mostly unfootnoted; each item above is also cited to its own page where one exists.

## Game notes (optional, suggestions only)
- Suggestion: model crystals as a primary crystal (sets colour and the base blade) plus up to two optional focusing crystals or modifiers, which matches the sources' "one required, three ideal".
- Suggestion: KOTOR-style crystal effects (deflection, ion damage against droids, stun, fire) could be modifiers, with rarity tied to power ("often, the rarer the crystal, the more powerful its effects").
- Suggestion: make cortosis a "shut-off" property (the blade drops for a short, variable time) and phrik, *beskar*, songsteel and ultrachrome a "parry, no shut-off" property; give ultrachrome a melt threshold under sustained contact.
- Suggestion: for an Old Republic setting (3,700–3,600 BBY), Sith characters use red synthetic crystals by default; Jedi gather natural crystals (Ilum, Tython meditation crystals) as a rite.
- Suggestion: training sabers could either stun (bondar) or do non-lethal damage, depending on which [conflict] version the developer picks.
